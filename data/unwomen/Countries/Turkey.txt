Why?
• Lowest Female Labor Force Participation Rate in the entire OECD: hovering at just ~34% to 36%
• Shocking Withdrawal from the Istanbul Convention: In March 2021, Turkey unilaterally withdrew by presidential decree from the landmark treaty named after its own largest city
• Ranked #127 out of 146 countries on the World Economic Forum's 2024 Global Gender Gap Index (lowest in the European/OECD region)
• Severe Childcare Deficit: Public childcare coverage is practically negligible, with only ~15% of children aged 3-5 enrolled in pre-primary care
• Unpaid care burden is five times higher for women than men (4.6 hours vs 55 minutes daily), locking millions of women out of economic independence

Hot Topics
• The unilateral 2021 withdrawal from the Istanbul Convention and surging rates of femicide and domestic violence
• Lowest female labor force participation in the OECD (~35%) driven by severe childcare shortages
• Government rhetoric urging women to have "at least three children" without providing public care infrastructure
• High proportion of informal employment among female domestic cleaners and agricultural care workers
• Severe currency depreciation and 70%+ inflation drastically reducing family purchasing power for care services

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Asserts that family is the foundation of Turkish society; constitutional duty under Article 41 to protect family peace and welfare.
• Highlights government cash incentives: childbirth grants, paid maternity leave (16 weeks), and partial wage support for half-time working mothers.
• Contends that national legislation—specifically Law No. 6284 (Protection of Family and Prevention of Violence Against Women)—provides robust protections without requiring the Istanbul Convention.
• Expanding early childhood education goals under the Ministry of National Education's 2023 Vision.
• Rejects external criticisms of its legal system as culturally disconnected and driven by political bias.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Reverse falling fertility rates (dropped to 1.51 in 2023, below replacement level) by promoting marriage and childbirth.
• Increase female employment to meet macroeconomic growth targets without eroding traditional family care values.
• Defend the controversial decision to exit the Istanbul Convention as a sovereign prerogative to protect family norms.
• Secure international funding for hosting over 3.6 million Syrian refugees, many of whom are vulnerable mothers and children.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Statutory paid maternity leave: 16 weeks at two-thirds wage replacement, with option for 6 months unpaid leave
✓ Law No. 6284 providing strong restraining orders and legal injunctions against domestic abusers
✓ Host to the world's largest refugee population (over 3.6 million Syrians), providing access to public maternal healthcare and primary schooling
✓ Expanding enrollment targets for 5-year-old pre-school education across public schools
✓ Half-time working rights with full-time pay subsidies for mothers returning from maternity leave until child begins elementary school

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Infamous Withdrawal from the Istanbul Convention (2021): Symbolic retreat from international standards on gender-based violence and care protection
✗ Lowest Female Labor Participation in OECD (35.6%): Over 11 million Turkish women are classified as economically inactive due to "household duties"
✗ Severe Childcare Shortage: Public daycare covers fewer than 5% of children aged 0 to 3, forcing women to quit jobs upon having children
✗ 5:1 Care Time Disparity: Turkish women perform 4.6 hours of unpaid care daily, compared to less than 1 hour (55 minutes) for men
✗ Ranked #127 on WEF Gender Gap Index: Lags far behind regional peers in economic participation (ranked 133rd)
✗ Rampant Femicide & Domestic Violence: Over 400 women killed by male partners/family members annually with weak judicial sentencing
✗ Informality in Domestic Care: Over 1 million domestic cleaners and in-home eldercare workers operate without formal contracts or SGK (Social Security) registration

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• 86% of non-working Turkish women identify household chores, childcare, or eldercare as the sole reason for not seeking employment.
• Culturally ingrained expectation that daughters and daughters-in-law provide full-time home care for elderly family members, resulting in immense stress and isolation.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Municipalities with 150+ female workers are legally required to provide a crèche (daycare), but enforcement is non-existent as companies routinely pay a tiny nominal fine instead.
• Private daycare costs exceed the national minimum wage, rendering work economically irrational for low- and middle-income mothers.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Widespread employment of undocumented migrant women from Central Asia (Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Georgia) and Syria as domestic maids and eldercare workers.
• Foreign domestic workers face irregular status, wage exploitation, and fear of deportation if they report abuse.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• #127: Turkey's ranking on the WEF Global Gender Gap Index 2024.
• 35.6%: Female labor force participation rate (worst in OECD, where the average is 65%).
• 1.51: Total fertility rate in 2023 (crashing despite presidential calls for at least 3 children).
• 15%: Pre-primary childcare enrollment rate for children aged 3 to 5 (OECD average is 83%).
• 400+: Femicides recorded annually in Turkey.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Demands that women bear at least three children while providing zero affordable public daycare centers, forcing millions into permanent economic inactivity.
• Claims Law 6284 is sufficient to protect women while police routinely encourage abused women to reconcile with violent husbands.
• Mandates workplace crèches for large employers on paper, but sets the non-compliance fine so low that corporations simply pay the fine rather than build the childcare center.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK TURKEY
-----------------------------------------------
1. Istanbul Convention POI: "Can the honorable delegate of Turkey explain how withdrawing from the Istanbul Convention—a treaty drafted in your own historic city to protect women from domestic violence—fosters safe conditions for women's economic and social mobility?"
   - Expected Turkey Defence: Claim that Law 6284 provides identical protections and that the Convention undermined family structures.
   - Philippine Counter: "If Law 6284 is sufficient, why did over 400 women lose their lives to domestic violence last year? Withdrawing from an international treaty sends a signal of state impunity, directly threatening women in the domestic care sphere."

2. OECD Participation Gap POI: "Turkey maintains the lowest female labor force participation rate in the OECD at just 35%, with 11 million women trapped in unpaid household chores. When will Turkey invest in universal municipal childcare rather than urging women to bear three children without public support?"

3. Workplace Crèche Enforcement POI: "Turkish labor regulations require businesses with over 150 women to provide daycare facilities, yet over 85% of eligible firms simply pay trivial administrative fines to evade compliance. Why has Turkey failed to enforce its own childcare laws?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM TURKEY
--------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has family breakdown issues due to high labor migration and divorce debates, whereas Turkey protects traditional family unity."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines protects family unity by empowering women economically, providing 105 days of paid maternity leave, and enacting Batas Kasambahay. Protecting the family does not mean trapping 65% of women outside the formal economy and withdrawing from landmark human rights treaties."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "A state policy that demands women have three children while providing zero public childcare is not a family policy—it is a recipe for female economic disenfranchisement."
• "We urge Turkey to rejoin the international consensus on women's safety, enforce its corporate daycare mandates, and recognize that women cannot participate in the modern economy without universal care infrastructure."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Strict international guidelines mandating corporate-funded or municipal-provided childcare for all medium and large enterprises with escalating non-compliance penalties.
• Multilateral reporting on national progress regarding legal protections against domestic violence as an essential prerequisite for women's economic mobility.
