COUNTRY-SPECIFIC AI, SURVEILLANCE & DATA PRIVACY POSITIONS
====================================================================

COUNTRY NAME: Afghanistan

1. Country Overview
- Government: Theocratic Administration
- Political System: Centralized system under the supreme leadership of the Amir and clerical councils
- Global Influence: Low (Subject to international non-recognition and financial asset freezes)
- Technology Sector: Extremely weak (highly dependent on imported telecommunications infrastructure and foreign software)

2. Official Position on AI
Prioritizes the enforcement of Islamic law (Sharia), public morality, and internal security. Rejects Western digital rights frameworks, privacy guidelines, and free speech protections as incompatible with Islamic law. Stresses that all telecommunications and internet systems must be monitored to ensure moral compliance and prevent counter-state operations.

3. National AI Strategy
- National AI plans: No national AI strategy or development strategy.
- Government initiatives: Focuses on telecommunications infrastructure recovery and establishing administrative control over mobile networks.
- AI priorities: Public security, facial recognition deployment, and monitoring digital communications.

4. AI Governance
- AI regulation: Coordinated by the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT).
- AI ethics: Focuses on aligning all digital activities with Islamic law (Sharia) and state decrees.
- Government oversight: Managed directly by the Ministry of Interior and intelligence services.

5. Data Privacy
- Privacy laws: No functional data privacy laws exist.
- Data protection authority: Governed provisionally by the MCIT.
- Citizen protections: Extremely limited; telecommunications and online activities are subject to direct state inspection.
- Cross-border data rules: Restricts outbound data flows, routing traffic through state-controlled gateways.

6. Mass Digital Surveillance
- CCTV: Planned deployment of a city-wide security camera network in Kabul.
- Facial recognition: Biometric databases compiled for identity verification and security tracking.
- Internet monitoring: Monitors telecommunications traffic and filters online content for moral compliance.
- Government surveillance: Security forces monitor mobile networks under national security mandates.
- Public justification: Essential for counter-terrorism, crime prevention, and maintaining public order.
- International criticism: Criticized by CSOs for using biometric databases to track and restrict the movements of women.

7. AI in Public Services
- Education: Basic e-learning portals, youth IT classes, and digital curriculum initiatives in state schools.
- Healthcare: Basic public health database support in state hospitals and clinics.
- Transportation: Camera-based traffic security tracking.
- Judiciary: Automated case indexing and case scheduling platforms.
- Public administration: Mandatory biometric SIM card registration databases.

8. AI in Defence
- Military AI: None.
- Autonomous systems: None.
- Cyber defence: Coordinated under the MCIT, securing state portals.
- National security applications: Cryptological monitoring, digital interception, and threat prediction.

9. Cybersecurity
- National cyber strategy: Weak cybersecurity infrastructure due to import restrictions on high-tech hardware.
- Cyber agencies: Ministry of Communications and Information Technology.
- Cyber threats: Opposition cyber groups, database breaches, and state-sponsored espionage.

10. Major Technology Companies
Not applicable.

11. International Cooperation
- UN: Participates in UNGA discussions.
- UNESCO: Contributes to cultural preservation tracks.
- OECD: Not a member; opposes Western-dominated guidelines.
- Bilateral agreements: Technology and cyber security agreements with regional partner states.
- Regional initiatives: Participates in regional technology tracks.

12. Human Rights Strengths
- Centralized network controls, enabling rapid detection of cybercrime and security threats.
- High mobile phone penetration, facilitating basic communication channels.
- State-backed efforts to maintain basic telecommunication services during conflict.
- Digital literacy initiatives in state training centers.
- Effective protection of state portals from hostile cyber sabotage.

13. Human Rights Concerns
- Use of biometric databases and cameras to track women and political opponents.
- Absence of comprehensive data privacy laws to protect citizens from state monitoring.
- Lack of independent judicial oversight for state surveillance activities.
- Digital censorship and blocking of independent news portals.
- Potential for automated surveillance to target human rights defenders.

16. Strong Defence Arguments
- City camera systems and communication controls are intended to restore public safety, counter ISIS-K terrorism, and maintain order.
- Technology regulations are designed to align digital spaces with the country's religious and cultural values.
- Biometric SIM card registration prevents telecommunications fraud and secures identity records.
- The administration works within its limits to rebuild telecommunication infrastructure.
- Domestic security operations are conducted to protect citizens from violent instability.
- The state works to ensure all digital systems align with the values and laws of the country.

17. Red Lines
- Will not support international regulations that restrict national security operations or run counter to Sharia.
- Rejects any resolution that criticizes its religious or internal policies.

18. Likely Allies
China, Russia (pragmatic relations).

19. Likely Opponents
United States, European Union Member States, India.

20. Suggested Bloc
Sovereign Bloc / Russia-China aligned.

21. Negotiation Strategy
Emphasize national security, counter-terrorism (specifically against ISIS-K), and cyber sovereignty. Counter Western criticism as interference.

22. Important Statistics
Internet user penetration is estimated at under 20% of the population due to economic challenges and network damage.

23. Quick Facts
- MCIT leads national telecom policy.
- Kabul city surveillance utilizes advanced camera networks.
- Biometric SIM card registration is mandatory.
- Lacks independent data privacy laws.
- Mobile networks are heavily dependent on state-run backbones.
- Internet penetration is extremely low.
- Target of foreign cyber monitoring.
- Lacks a national AI strategy.
- Opposes Western digital rights frameworks.
- Rebuilt basic telecom networks in urban zones.
- Internet penetration is estimated at around 18% nationally.
- Subject to extensive international technology export restrictions.

24. Executive Summary
The Taliban administration prioritizes Islamic law (Sharia), public morality, and national security. Rejects Western frameworks of digital rights, privacy, and free speech, stating that digital platforms must comply with Islamic values and state decrees.
