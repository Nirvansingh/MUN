UNHRC RESOURCE GUIDE: KEY UN RESOLUTIONS
================================================================

This document lists the most important UN General Assembly (UNGA) and Human Rights Council (UNHRC) resolutions regarding digital privacy, artificial intelligence, and mass surveillance.

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. UNGA RESOLUTION A/RES/68/167 (18 December 2013)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: The Right to Privacy in the Digital Age.
• Key Focus: First major UNGA resolution addressing mass digital surveillance.
• Key Elements:
  - Establishes that the same rights people have offline must also be protected online, including the right to privacy.
  - Expresses deep concern over the negative impact of surveillance, interception of communications, and data collection on human rights.
  - Calls on states to review their laws, procedures, and practices regarding communication interception to ensure compliance with human rights.

----------------------------------------------------------------
2. UNHRC RESOLUTION HRC 34/7 (23 March 2017)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: The Right to Privacy in the Digital Age.
• Key Focus: Expanding UNHRC mandate and establishing the Special Rapporteur on the right to privacy.
• Key Elements:
  - Encourages states to promote encryption and anonymity as necessary tools for the protection of privacy and freedom of expression.
  - Identifies that business enterprises have a responsibility to respect human rights, in line with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights.

----------------------------------------------------------------
3. UNHRC RESOLUTION HRC 48/4 (7 October 2021)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: Right to Privacy in the Digital Age - AI and Biometrics.
• Key Focus: Specifically addresses the rapid deployment of artificial intelligence and biometric surveillance.
• Key Elements:
  - Calls for a temporary ban or strict moratorium on the sale and use of biometric technologies (facial recognition) in public spaces until human rights safeguards are established.
  - Stresses that algorithmic systems must not lead to discriminatory profiling or decisions in social service delivery.

----------------------------------------------------------------
4. UNGA RESOLUTION A/RES/73/179 (17 December 2018)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: The Right to Privacy in the Digital Age.
• Key Focus: Data commercialization and state database monitoring.
• Key Elements:
  - Urges states to establish national regulatory frameworks for data brokers to prevent unchecked commercial profiling of citizens.
  - Reaffirms that metadata collection (location, contacts, duration) can be as invasive as content interception and must be regulated.
