AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: RED (Extreme Agenda-Based Target)
Delegate: Myra Negi

Why?
• Ranked #145 out of 146 countries on the World Economic Forum's 2024 Global Gender Gap Index (second-to-last globally)
• Female Labor Force Participation Rate (FLFPR) sits at an abysmal ~21% to 23%
• Unpaid care work disparity is among the widest in the world: Pakistani women spend 10.2 times more time on unpaid care and domestic labor than men
• Over 70% of economically active women are in informal, home-based, or agricultural care work, entirely excluded from social security, minimum wage laws, and health insurance
• High maternal mortality (186 per 100,000 live births) and systemic lack of public early childhood and maternal healthcare infrastructure

Hot Topics
• Extreme time poverty of rural women performing unpaid agricultural, fuel collection, and family care labor
• Exclusion of domestic workers and Home-Based Workers (HBWs) from formal provincial labor legislation
• Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP) / Ehsaas social safety nets: lifeline cash transfers versus structural empowerment
• Child marriage rates (18% of girls married before 18) directly terminating female educational and social mobility
• The Sindh Home-Based Workers Act (2018) and Punjab Domestic Workers Act (2019): progressive paper laws versus near-zero real-world enforcement

Expected Debate Frequency: High

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Highlights the Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP), providing direct unconditional cash transfers to over 9 million underprivileged female heads of household.
• Cites landmark provincial legislation: Sindh Home-Based Workers Act (2018) and Punjab Domestic Workers Act (2019) recognizing domestic workers' statutory rights.
• Defends cultural and religious traditions of family solidarity and multi-generational domestic support.
• Demands climate justice and multilateral financial support, noting that climate disasters (e.g., the 2022 catastrophic floods) disproportionately destroy care infrastructure and burden women.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Secure international development grants and climate adaptation funds to restore rural care and sanitation infrastructure.
• Defend the country's sovereign social and religious fabric from what it characterizes as Western cultural imposition.
• Highlight progress in conditional cash transfers (BISP) to deflect from low international gender equality rankings.
• Prevent binding multilateral labor conventions that require impossible enforcement mechanisms in rural informal sectors.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Benazir Income Support Programme (BISP) / Ehsaas: One of South Asia's largest targeted social safety nets, empowering women via direct digital cash transfers
✓ Punjab Domestic Workers Act (2019) legally prohibiting the employment of children under 15 as domestic workers and mandating employment contracts
✓ Sindh Home-Based Workers Act (2018) formally extending labor rights and social security registration to home-based female pieceworkers
✓ Pro-women legislation including the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act (amended 2022)
✓ Constitutional commitment under Article 25 (Equality of Citizens) and Article 34 (Full Participation of Women in National Life)

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Ranked #145 out of 146 on the 2024 WEF Global Gender Gap Report (ranked 145th in Economic Participation and Opportunity)
✗ Female Labor Force Participation Rate remains stagnant at ~21-23%, representing massive female exclusion from the formal economy
✗ 10.2:1 Care Time Disparity: Pakistani women perform an average of 10.2 hours of unpaid domestic and care work for every 1 hour performed by men
✗ Over 70% of female workers are in informal, unregulated home-based or agricultural labor without written contracts, pensions, or healthcare
✗ Rampant Child Labor in Domestic Service: Tragic, high-profile cases of torture and death of young domestic maidservants ("Tayyaba", "Rizwana") in affluent households
✗ Near-zero enforcement of provincial domestic worker laws: Fewer than 1% of domestic workers are formally registered with social security institutions
✗ High maternal mortality and lack of rural childcare facilities force girls to drop out of school to care for younger siblings

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Rural women spend up to 14 hours a day on unpaid domestic chores: collecting firewood, hauling water from distant wells, tending livestock, and cooking over biomass stoves, severely limiting physical health and literacy.
• The economic value of women's unpaid care work in Pakistan is estimated at over $45 billion annually (~15% of national GDP), yet is treated as economically valueless.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Formal, regulated public childcare is virtually nonexistent in rural areas and unaffordable for working-class families in urban centers.
• Eldercare is entirely relegated to the private, unpaid domestic sphere, with daughters-in-law bearing the cultural expectation of full-time eldercare.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Thousands of young girls and women work as informal domestic maids in private homes, often enduring physical confinement, wage theft, and abuse without labor inspection access.
• Has not ratified ILO Convention 189.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• #145: Pakistan's global ranking on the WEF Global Gender Gap Index 2024.
• 21.4%: Female labor force participation rate in Pakistan (among the lowest in the world).
• 10.2x: Multiplier of time spent by women on unpaid care work relative to men.
• 70%+: Proportion of women in informal home-based and unpaid family agricultural labor.
• 18%: Percentage of Pakistani girls married before their 18th birthday.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Touts provincial legislation (Sindh and Punjab Acts) at the UN while domestic workers in major cities continue to suffer horrific abuse with zero state labor inspections.
• Promotes BISP cash transfers as female empowerment while the actual monthly stipend (~9,000 PKR / $32) is insufficient to lift families out of structural poverty.
• Claims to protect women's constitutional rights while failing to allocate sufficient provincial funding for public childcare centers or rural piped water infrastructure.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK PAKISTAN
-------------------------------------------------
1. Unpaid Care & Labor Exclusion POI: "Can the honorable delegate of Pakistan explain how Pakistani women can achieve economic mobility when Pakistan ranks second-to-last globally (#145) on the Gender Gap Index, and women perform over ten times more unpaid domestic care labor than men?"
   - Expected Pakistan Defence: Point to BISP cash transfers, provincial home-based worker acts, and Islamic rights frameworks.
   - Philippine Counter: "BISP provides a survival stipend, not economic mobility. Without universal rural water infrastructure and childcare, women remain bound by unpaid time poverty. When will Pakistan ratify ILO Convention 189?"

2. Child Domestic Servitude POI: "Despite the Punjab Domestic Workers Act, horrific cases of physical abuse and death of child domestic maids in wealthy Pakistani households continue to make international headlines. What concrete enforcement mechanism has Pakistan established to inspect private homes and end the brutal exploitation of domestic care workers?"

3. Implementation Gap POI: "The Sindh Home-Based Workers Act was passed in 2018, yet six years later, what percentage of the estimated 5 million home-based care and craft workers have actually received formal social security registration and health cards?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM PAKISTAN
----------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has high rates of female labor migration that split families, whereas Pakistan prioritizes the unity of the family unit."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines respects family values by legally recognizing, protecting, and investing in them. Our women hold equal leadership, 105 days of paid maternity leave, and formal legal protections under Batas Kasambahay. Protecting the family does not mean confining women to 14 hours of unpaid manual domestic chores with a 21% labor participation rate."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "We cannot speak of poverty eradication in South Asia while millions of women spend their entire lives hauling water and grinding grain without a single rupee in compensation, recognized status, or access to a pension."
• "Passing laws on paper in provincial assemblies is meaningless if not a single labor inspector enters a household to protect vulnerable domestic workers."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Dedicated international funding for basic rural domestic infrastructure (piped water, rural electrification) directly targeting female time poverty.
• Global support for digital registration and social security micro-contributions for informal Home-Based Workers (HBWs).
