AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: RED (Extreme Agenda-Based Target)

Why?
• World's lowest total fertility rate in recorded history (0.72 in 2023, dropping below 0.6 in Seoul)—an existential demographic crisis driven by care burdens
• Widest gender wage gap in the entire OECD for 27 consecutive years: 31.1% (women earn ~69% of men's wages)
• "Gyeongdan-nyeo" phenomenon: millions of educated women forced to abandon their careers permanently upon marriage or childbirth
• South Korea piloted importing 100 Filipina domestic workers in 2024, but faced intense controversy over attempts by politicians to exempt foreign domestic workers from the national minimum wage
• Highly patriarchal family structure where women perform nearly 4 times more unpaid care than men despite working the longest market hours in the OECD

Hot Topics
• The 2024 Seoul Foreign Domestic Worker Pilot Program and controversial minimum wage debates
• Existential fertility collapse (0.72) driven by astronomical private education ("hagwon") and housing costs
• The "Motherhood Penalty" and systemic career interruption of college-educated women
• Over 31% gender wage gap—worst in the OECD
• Anti-feminist political backlash and threats to abolish the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family

Expected Debate Frequency: Extremely High / Prime Target

BASIC INFO
----------
Official Name: Republic of Korea (ROK)
Capital: Seoul
Government: Unitary Presidential Constitutional Republic

Care Economy Model: Hyper-competitive Market / Confucian Patriarchal Family Care

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Declares a national population emergency; expanding state childcare cash handouts ("Parental Allowance" of up to 1 million KRW/month).
• Increasing paid parental leave duration from 12 to 18 months for both parents under the Equal Employment Opportunity Act.
• Expanding state-subsidized after-school care programs ("Neulbom School") to keep elementary students supervised until 8:00 PM.
• Piloting the introduction of Southeast Asian (specifically Filipina) foreign domestic caregivers to lower household care costs for dual-income working couples.
• Emphasizes expanding work-life balance initiatives, telecommuting, and flexible hours in private enterprises.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Reverse catastrophic demographic decline that threatens to halve the working-age population by 2050.
• Keep high-skilled female talent in the labor market without requiring sweeping reductions in corporate overtime hours.
• Reduce the cost of in-home domestic help for Korean dual-income families through targeted foreign labor migration.
• Defend the administration's controversial restructuring of the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family against international condemnation.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Cash subsidies for families: Generous Parental Allowance offering up to 1 million KRW ($750) monthly for infants under 1 year
✓ Statutory parental leave up to 18 months per parent with up to 80% wage replacement (up to statutory ceiling)
✓ "Neulbom School" nationwide initiative providing comprehensive public after-school care until 8:00 PM
✓ Highest rate of tertiary educational attainment among young women in the OECD (over 75% of women aged 25-34 hold college degrees)
✓ Comprehensive public healthcare system covering extensive prenatal and maternal health services

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ World's Lowest Fertility Rate (0.72 in 2023, forecasted 0.68 in 2024): Korean women are on an effective "birth strike" due to care burdens
✗ Worst Gender Wage Gap in OECD (31.1%): Stagnant for nearly three decades; women make up less than 6% of corporate board directors
✗ Career Interruption ("Gyeongdan-nyeo"): Nearly 40% of married women quit their careers due to childbirth, childcare, or eldercare
✗ Exploitative Wage Attempts on Migrant Care Workers: Seoul politicians actively proposed paying Filipina domestic workers below the Korean statutory minimum wage, creating bilateral tensions with the Philippines
✗ Extreme Unpaid Care Burden: Korean women spend 3 hours and 33 minutes daily on unpaid care, compared to just 54 minutes for men
✗ Severe Corporate "Overwork Culture" (Yageun): Culture of mandatory unpaid overtime and evening socializing ("hoesik") penalizes anyone with family obligations
✗ Political Backlash: Government attempts to dismantle the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family have severely damaged institutional gender mainstreaming

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Despite holding higher college graduation rates than men, women perform over 80% of all household and family care.
• Mothers spend an average of 4.5 hours daily on childcare and education management, driven by hyper-competitive "hagwon" (cram school) culture.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Government has poured over $280 billion into fertility and childcare subsidies over 16 years with zero positive effect on birth rates, because money cannot compensate for career termination.
• Eldercare faces immense strain: South Korea has the highest elderly poverty rate in the OECD (~40%), leaving elderly women deeply vulnerable.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• In 2024, launched a pilot program importing 100 Filipina domestic workers to Seoul.
• The initiative was mired in controversy when prominent politicians argued foreign domestic workers should be exempt from the 9,860 KRW ($7.30) national minimum wage, drawing fierce pushback from the Philippine government, trade unions, and civil society.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 0.72: South Korea's total fertility rate in 2023 (replacement rate is 2.1).
• 31.1%: OECD's widest gender wage gap (held by South Korea for 27 years).
• $280 Billion: Public funds spent on fertility subsidies since 2006 without reversing the fertility collapse.
• 40.4%: Relative poverty rate among South Korea's elderly population (OECD highest).
• 54 minutes: Time spent daily by Korean men on unpaid domestic work and care.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Pours billions of dollars into family cash handouts while refusing to enforce labor laws against corporations that fire pregnant women or force 60-hour workweeks.
• Extols gender equality while conservative politicians actively attempt to abolish the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family.
• Seeks to solve its domestic care crisis by importing Filipino care workers while attempting to pay them sub-minimum wages.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK SOUTH KOREA
----------------------------------------------------
1. Minimum Wage & Migrant Workers POI: "Can the honorable delegate of South Korea explain why official proposals in Seoul sought to exempt Filipina domestic workers from the national minimum wage? Does South Korea believe that the human dignity and labor value of a caregiver depends on the color of their passport?"
   - Expected South Korea Defence: Argue that the proposals were private opinions, that the pilot currently complies with the minimum wage, and that lower wages would help more working mothers.
   - Philippine Counter: "The fact that wage discrimination was ever entertained demonstrates that South Korea views foreign care workers as cheap domestic commodities rather than equal labor partners. Care labor cannot be devalued to subsidize broken domestic corporate models."

2. Gender Wage Gap POI: "For 27 consecutive years, South Korea has maintained the widest gender wage gap in the OECD at over 31%. When will your government move beyond token cash handouts and dismantle the structural corporate penalties that force women into 'Gyeongdan-nyeo' career termination?"

3. Fertility Crisis POI: "With the world's lowest fertility rate at 0.72, is it not evident that cash handouts of 1 million won cannot fix an economic system where working mothers face 60-hour workweeks and four times the unpaid care burden of their spouses?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM SOUTH KOREA
-------------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has lower female employment rates and exports its women rather than providing sufficient domestic jobs."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines is proud of our professional care workers who sustain families worldwide, but we do not allow them to be exploited. We protected our workers by insisting on the full Korean minimum wage for the Seoul pilot. More importantly, Philippine women hold equal senior managerial positions and our gender wage gap is a fraction of South Korea's 31%."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "South Korea's fertility rate of 0.72 is not an anomaly; it is a global warning. It proves that when society leaves the entire burden of the care economy on women's shoulders while demanding 60-hour corporate workweeks, women will simply stop having children."
• "We remind South Korea: you cannot import foreign domestic workers to fix your care deficit while seeking to deny them the very minimum wage protections your own laws guarantee."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Strict prohibition against nationality-based wage discrimination for domestic and care workers in national legislation.
• Binding multilateral mechanisms penalizing corporate workplace discrimination against pregnant workers and mothers returning from parental leave.
