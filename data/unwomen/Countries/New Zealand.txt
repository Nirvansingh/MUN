AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: GREEN (Global Pay Equity Pioneer / Pacific Ally)

Why?
• World-leading pioneer in Care Work Pay Equity: Enacted the Equal Pay Amendment Act (2020) following the historic Kristine Bartlett aged care court case, establishing statutory pathways to revalue female-dominated care work
• Statutory Paid Parental Leave of 26 weeks at up to 100% of previous earnings (capped at statutory maximum)
• High female labor force participation rate (~70%) and historic female political representation (achieved 50% female parliament in 2022)
• Universal "20 Hours ECE": 20 hours of free early childhood education per week for all children aged 3, 4, and 5
• Significant Filipino care diaspora working in hospitals, disability support, and dairy farming sectors

Hot Topics
• Historic Pay Equity settlements: Raising wages for aged care, healthcare assistants, and early childhood teachers
• High cost of early childhood education beyond the state-subsidized 20 free hours
• Pay equity settlements under scrutiny following fiscal tightening by the National-led coalition government
• Māori and Pacific whānau care traditions and health inequities
• Skilled migrant pathways for international nurses and caregivers

Expected Debate Frequency: Medium-Low / Progressive Partner

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Advocates for genuine "Pay Equity": Revaluing care professions that have been historically undervalued because they are predominantly performed by women.
• Showcases the Equal Pay Act: Enabling unions and care workers to file sector-wide pay equity claims compared against male-dominated comparator professions.
• Highlights 26 weeks of statutory paid parental leave and the "20 Hours ECE" early childhood education scheme.
• Promotes flexible working arrangements under the Employment Relations Act.
• Supports ethical international recruitment of healthcare workers while providing clear residency pathways.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Champion pay equity and gender equality across the Pacific and Commonwealth.
• Attract and retain international healthcare and aged care workers, particularly from the Philippines and Pacific states.
• Manage fiscal costs associated with historic pay equity settlements in the public health sector.
• Ensure culturally safe care infrastructure for indigenous Māori and Pacific communities.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Equal Pay Amendment Act (2020): Global benchmark allowing care workers to claim equal pay for work of equal value
✓ 26 weeks of government-funded Paid Parental Leave (PPL)
✓ 20 Hours ECE: 20 hours of free early childhood education per week for all children aged 3 to 5
✓ High female political representation: Achieved 50% female parliament in 2022; first country where women won the right to vote (1893)
✓ Clear, fast-track "Green List" residency pathways for registered nurses, midwives, and specialized caregivers

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Government Scrutiny of Pay Equity: Recent fiscal policy shifts have slowed down pending pay equity claims for social care workers
✗ High Childcare Costs: Out-of-pocket childcare fees for infants under age 3 remain high in Auckland and Wellington
✗ Aged Care Staff Shortages: Nursing homes face chronic bed closures due to staff shortages, forcing elderly citizens into hospital waitlists
✗ Māori & Pacific Care Disparities: Indigenous and Pacific women face lower median wages, higher rates of unpaid family care, and poorer health outcomes
✗ Living Costs & Housing Affordability: Skyrocketing housing costs erode the benefits of paid parental leave for low-income families

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 26 Weeks: Duration of statutory paid parental leave.
• 20 Hours: Free early childhood education provided per week for children aged 3 to 5.
• 50%: Proportion of female MPs in New Zealand's Parliament.
• 2020: Year of the landmark Equal Pay Amendment Act revaluing care professions.

PHILIPPINES-NEW ZEALAND RELATIONS
---------------------------------
• Highly positive relationship: Thousands of Filipino nurses and healthcare assistants are settled in New Zealand, recognized for their vital contributions to the public health system.
• Both nations strongly support the 5R Framework and pay equity in the care economy.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "New Zealand has proven that pay equity is not an abstract concept—it is a legal mechanism to correct decades of underpayment in care work. When you pay care workers fair wages, quality of care rises and the gender wage gap falls."
• "We urge member states to adopt statutory pay equity frameworks that allow care workers to be evaluated and compensated based on their true societal worth."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Model Clause on Pay Equity for Care Work: Requiring member states to establish statutory legal mechanisms for care workers to benchmark their wages against comparable male-dominated occupations.
