AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: RED (Strong Agenda-Based Target)

Why?
• Only advanced OECD economy without statutory federal paid parental or maternity leave
• Childcare costs consume up to 30% to 40% of average family income in urban centers
• Heavy reliance on informal and underpaid domestic and care labor (predominantly immigrant women of color)
• Gender wage gap remains persistent at 16% (women earn ~84 cents for every dollar men earn, lower for women of color)
• Promotes private-market care models while resisting binding international labor standards (unratified ILO C189, CEDAW)

Hot Topics
• Lack of federal paid family and medical leave (FMLA is unpaid and covers only ~56% of workers)
• High cost of early childhood care ("childcare cliff" after pandemic relief expiration)
• Non-ratification of CEDAW and ILO Convention 189 (Domestic Workers)
• Exclusion of home health aides and domestic workers from standard labor protections under Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) historical exemptions
• State-level reproductive health restrictions impacting women's economic participation

Expected Debate Frequency: Extremely High

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Advocates for private-sector solutions, employer-provided childcare tax credits, and flexible market-based work options.
• Opposes heavy federal mandates, universal public entitlement schemes, or international binding regulations that interfere with state-level labor laws and free enterprise.
• Emphasizes female entrepreneurship, STEM workforce development, and economic deregulation as the true drivers of women's mobility.
• Resists supranational oversight of domestic labor markets; has not ratified CEDAW or ILO Convention 189.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Protect domestic corporate competitiveness and avoid federally mandated employer costs.
• Promote American corporate leadership and market-driven childcare public-private partnerships (PPPs).
• Defend the US record on high female educational attainment and senior corporate leadership.
• Resist binding multilateral taxation or wealth redistribution mechanisms proposed by developing blocs.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ World leader in female corporate leadership, entrepreneurship, and venture capital financing for women-led startups
✓ High proportion of women in higher education, professional degrees, and specialized professions
✓ Federal Child and Dependent Care Tax Credit (CDCTC) and employer childcare tax incentives
✓ Robust private sector and philanthropic investments in caregiving technology and eldercare innovation
✓ Equal Pay Act (1963) and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act protecting workplace discrimination

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Only industrialized country in the world without a statutory national paid maternity/parental leave mandate
✗ Infamous "Childcare Cliff": Over 70,000 childcare programs face closure due to lack of permanent federal funding
✗ Childcare costs exceed college tuition in over 30 states, averaging $11,000 to $18,000 per child annually
✗ Home health aides and childcare workers earn near-poverty median wages (~$15/hour), 90% female, often lacking health insurance
✗ Massive racial care gap: Black and Latina women experience significantly wider wage and pension penalties from caregiving
✗ Has refused to ratify CEDAW (joined only by Iran, Somalia, Sudan) and ILO Convention 189

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• American women spend an average of 4.1 hours daily on unpaid care and domestic labor, compared to 2.4 hours for men.
• Over 53 million Americans act as unpaid caregivers for family members, predominantly elderly parents, costing caregivers an estimated $522 billion in lost lifetime wages and retirement security.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Childcare is largely privatized and unaffordable for working-class families; public pre-K exists in only a handful of states.
• Eldercare relies on Medicare (which covers acute care, not long-term daily personal care) and Medicaid (requiring individuals to spend down into poverty to qualify).

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Domestic workers were historically excluded from the National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) and Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). While some wage rules now apply, home care workers lack collective bargaining rights under federal law.
• High reliance on undocumented and visa-tied immigrant female care workers who face precarity and wage theft.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 0: Weeks of federally guaranteed paid maternity leave (only 27% of private-sector workers have paid family leave through employers).
• $11,582: Average annual cost of infant care in the US, exceeding median single-mother income by over 35%.
• 84%: Women's median earnings relative to men (drops to 66% for Black women, 58% for Hispanic women).
• 14%: Proportion of female workers who leave the labor force or reduce hours due to lack of affordable eldercare.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Champions women's economic empowerment globally through USAID and development programs while refusing to guarantee basic paid maternity leave for its own citizens.
• Lectures multilateral forums on human rights while being one of only four nations refusing to ratify CEDAW.
• Claims to support family values while leaving families to bear astronomical childcare costs with zero federal safety net.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK THE USA
------------------------------------------------
1. Hard / Factual POI: "Can the honorable delegate of the United States explain how American women can achieve genuine economic mobility when the US remains the only OECD nation with zero statutory paid maternity leave, forcing one in four mothers to return to work within two weeks of giving birth?"
   - Expected US Defence: Point to the FMLA, state-level paid leave programs (e.g., California, New York), and private-sector benefits.
   - Philippine Counter: "FMLA is unpaid and excludes 44% of workers, and state programs cover only a fraction of the population. Without a federal floor, economic mobility is dictated by socioeconomic privilege, not equal opportunity."

2. Diplomatic / Systemic POI: "Given that the United States advocates for global care protections, will the US commit today to ratifying CEDAW and ILO Convention 189 to grant domestic workers federal collective bargaining rights?"
   - Expected US Defence: Argue that US federal and state statutes already provide strong anti-discrimination protections without requiring treaty ratification.
   - Philippine Counter: "Yet federal labor law continues to deny domestic workers collective bargaining under the NLRA. If your protections are superior, what justifies avoiding international accountability?"

3. Follow-up POI on Childcare: "With over 70,000 childcare centers at risk of closure due to expiring federal relief, how does the US plan to prevent millions of working mothers from being pushed out of the workforce?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM USA
-----------------------------------------------

Our labor migration is fully regulated by our Department of Migrant Workers. Unlike the US, we have enshrined gender equality into our national constitution and ratified ILO C189."
• Philippine Counter-POI to USA: "Will the US match our statutory 105 days of paid maternity leave, or will American mothers remain reliant on employer discretion?"

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "A market-only approach to the care economy guarantees that only the affluent access care, while the women who provide that care are trapped in working poverty."
• "We urge our American colleagues to join the rest of the civilized world in recognizing paid maternity leave not as an employer perk, but as a universal human right."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International standard for minimum 14 weeks paid maternity leave (ILO C183 compliance).
• Inclusion of domestic and home care workers in all national collective bargaining and labor relations legislation.
