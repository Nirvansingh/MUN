Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Delegate: Mannat Agarwal

Why?
• High female labor force participation rate (~68%) supported by world-renowned universal public care infrastructure ("École Maternelle" and "Crèches")
• Historically led the European Union in fertility rates (~1.8), though recently declining to 1.68 in 2023
• Controversial 2023 Pension Reform: Raising the legal retirement age from 62 to 64 disproportionately penalized women who took career breaks for unpaid care work
• Persistent gender wage gap (~14.9%) and gender pension gap (~30%) despite strict statutory equal pay reporting ("Index de l'égalité professionnelle")
• Generous parental leave compensation is relatively low, leading to low take-up by fathers

Hot Topics
• The 2023 Pension Reform and its discriminatory impact on mothers with fragmented care career paths
• Shortage of municipal nursery slots: Over 200,000 public childcare ("crèche") places missing nationwide
• Paid Paternity Leave expansion (doubled to 28 days in 2021) versus stubborn cultural care imbalances
• Reliance on migrant domestic cleaners and home health aides from North and West Africa
• "Diplomatie Féministe": France's international foreign policy priority championing global gender equality

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Champions a "Feminist Foreign Policy" committing 85% of official development assistance (ODA) to gender equality by 2025.
• Highlights the universal "École Maternelle" providing free public preschool education for all children from age 3.
• Promotes the "Index de l'égalité professionnelle," mandating companies with 50+ employees to publish gender equality scores with financial penalties for non-compliance.
• Offers 16 weeks of paid maternity leave (at 100% of capped earnings) and doubled paternity leave to 28 days in 2021.
• Investing billions in a public care guarantee ("Service Public de la Petite Enfance") to create 200,000 new nursery spots by 2030.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Maintain fiscal discipline and pension solvency while preserving social peace following massive pension reform protests.
• Position France and the European Union as the premier multilateral champions of universal public childcare and reproductive rights.
• Address labor shortages in early childhood education and eldercare without creating inflationary wage spirals.
• Defend the 2023 pension law against domestic and international allegations of female pension penalties.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Free, universal public preschool ("École Maternelle") for all children aged 3 to 6 with near 100% enrollment
✓ 28 days of statutory paid paternity leave (7 mandatory), with over 70% national take-up rate
✓ High female labor force participation (68.2%), significantly above the OECD average
✓ Enshrined the guaranteed freedom to voluntary abortion into the French Constitution in March 2024 (world first)
✓ Mandatory corporate gender equality index with state fines of up to 1% of total payroll for non-compliant companies

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ The 2023 Pension Reform Injustice: Raising the retirement age to 64 penalizes women who already worked longer than men to compensate for maternity career breaks, widening the 30% gender pension gap
✗ Public Nursery Shortage: Approximately 200,000 missing childcare slots; over 50% of working parents cannot secure a municipal crèche place
✗ 14.9% Gender Wage Gap: Persistent across the private sector, widening to nearly 25% when including part-time work
✗ Underpaid Care Workforce: Nursery workers and in-home elderly caregivers ("aides à domicile") earn near minimum wage with physically grueling schedules, leading to a recruitment crisis
✗ Low Parental Leave Allowance: Shared parental leave ("PreParE") offers only ~€448 per month, making it financially impossible for the higher-earning spouse (usually the father) to take leave

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• French women spend an average of 3 hours and 26 minutes daily on unpaid domestic work and care, compared to 1 hour and 50 minutes for men.
• Women perform 65% of all unpaid household and childcare labor, despite near-equal full-time employment rates.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• World-class preschool from age 3, but infant care (ages 0-3) is deeply fragmented between expensive certified childminders ("assistantes maternelles") and scarce public crèches.
• Eldercare facilities ("EHPAD") were shaken by the "Les Fossoyeurs" (The Gravediggers) commercial care home scandal in 2022, exposing severe neglect of elderly residents and exploitation of care workers in privatized nursing homes.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Broad use of the "Chèque Emploi Service Universel" (CESU), allowing families to declare domestic cleaners and nannies with a 50% income tax credit, formalizing domestic work.
• However, many undocumented migrant women from North and West Africa remain trapped in informal domestic work without papers or labor rights.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 68.2%: Female labor force participation rate in France.
• 30.2%: The Gender Pension Gap in France (women receive on average 30% lower pensions than men).
• 200,000: Deficit of early childhood childcare slots needed to satisfy working family demand.
• €448: Monthly allowance for shared parental leave (PreParE), which 99% of fathers refuse due to low wage replacement.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Preaches a "Feminist Foreign Policy" globally while passing a domestic pension reform that independent experts and unions confirmed disproportionately hurts women with caregiving interruptions.
• Mandates corporate equality indexes while privatized eldercare homes (EHPADs) cut staffing to generate corporate profits at the expense of underpaid female caregivers.
• Offers "shared parental leave" while setting the compensation at a poverty-level €448/month, guaranteeing that fathers do not take it.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK FRANCE
-----------------------------------------------
1. Pension Reform Injustice POI: "Can the honorable delegate of France explain how its self-proclaimed 'Feminist Foreign Policy' reconciles with its domestic 2023 pension reform, which raised the retirement age to 64 and directly penalized women who took career breaks for childbirth and family care, further widening France's 30% gender pension gap?"
   - Expected France Defence: Argue that pension reforms included compensatory quarters for child-rearing and a minimum pension floor of €1,200.
   - Philippine Counter: "The French High Council for Family, Children and Age (HCFEA) explicitly concluded that the reform forces women to work several months longer than men to achieve a full pension. When the state penalizes mothers at retirement for taking care breaks, it devalues the care economy."

2. Parental Leave Compensation POI: "Why does France's shared parental leave (PreParE) offer only €448 per month, ensuring that over 90% of fathers refuse to take it and leaving mothers to bear the full career interruption?"

3. Eldercare Quality POI: "Following the systemic neglect scandals in privatized nursing homes (EHPADs), what concrete statutory measures has France taken to end the commercialization of eldercare and protect underpaid care workers?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM FRANCE
--------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has high informal sector employment and lower female labor participation rates."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines is proud of our pioneering Batas Kasambahay, our 105 days of fully paid maternity leave, and our constitutional guarantee of gender equality. Unlike France, we do not penalize women in retirement for having cared for children, and we have fully ratified ILO Convention 189."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "A truly progressive care economy does not punish women when they reach their senior years. Retirement systems must formally credit years of caregiving as full economic contributions."
• "Universal public care infrastructure is the gold standard, but it must be matched by dignified wages for the care workers and fair parental leave compensation that allows fathers to share the care."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International standard establishing "Care Credits" in public pension systems, guaranteeing that career breaks for child and eldercare do not reduce retirement pension calculations.
• Strict regulatory caps and quality audits on commercial, for-profit eldercare facilities to prevent corporate neglect of care recipients and caregivers.
