COUNTRY-SPECIFIC AI, SURVEILLANCE & DATA PRIVACY POSITIONS
====================================================================

COUNTRY NAME: Syria

1. Country Overview
- Government: Presidential Republic
- Political System: Centralized system with executive power held by the President
- Global Influence: Moderate-Low (Regional actor, active partnerships with Russia and Iran)
- Technology Sector: Highly damaged (focused on basic telecom rehabilitation and communication security)

2. Official Position on AI
Focuses on national security, state survival, and counter-terrorism after more than a decade of conflict. Rejects Western criticism of its digital policies as violations of sovereignty and claims that all telecommunications regulations are designed to combat terrorism and foreign intervention.

3. National AI Strategy
- National AI plans: No national AI policy or development strategy due to conflict.
- Government initiatives: Focuses on digital infrastructure reconstruction and security monitoring in government-controlled areas.
- AI priorities: Public safety, database security, and counter-terrorism monitoring.

4. AI Governance
- AI regulation: No active regulatory framework for AI.
- AI ethics: Supports international guidelines on ethics, emphasizing security and state stability.
- Government oversight: Managed directly through the Ministry of Communications and Technology.

5. Data Privacy
- Privacy laws: Data privacy laws are subordinate to national security laws and emergency decrees.
- Data protection authority: Governed provisionally by the Ministry of Communications and Technology.
- Citizen protections: Extremely limited; communications and personal records are subject to direct state monitoring under security mandates.
- Cross-border data rules: Restricts data flows outside the country, routing traffic through state-controlled gateways.

6. Mass Digital Surveillance
- CCTV: Deployed in metropolitan public transit and border zones by security forces.
- Facial recognition: Limited deployment at government checkpoints and border crossings.
- Internet monitoring: Telecommunications traffic is intercepted by security services under national security laws.
- Government surveillance: Coordinated by intelligence directorates under national security decrees.
- Public justification: Essential for counter-terrorism, national security, and maintaining public order.
- International criticism: Criticized by CSOs and international bodies for electronic interception and tracking of opposition members.

7. AI in Public Services
- Education: Basic e-learning portals, youth IT classes, and digital curriculum initiatives in government schools.
- Healthcare: Basic public health database support in state hospitals and clinics.
- Transportation: Camera-based traffic security tracking.
- Judiciary: Automated case indexing and case scheduling platforms.
- Public administration: E-government portals automating identity registration and tax collections.

8. AI in Defence
- Military AI: None.
- Autonomous systems: None.
- Cyber defence: Coordinated under the Ministry of Communications and Technology, securing state portals.
- National security applications: Cryptological monitoring, digital interception, and threat prediction.

9. Cybersecurity
- National cyber strategy: Guided by emergency decrees focusing on network security.
- Cyber agencies: Ministry of Communications and Technology.
- Cyber threats: Opposition cyber groups, database breaches, and state-sponsored espionage.

10. Major Technology Companies
Not applicable.

11. International Cooperation
- UN: Active participant in UNGA and UNHRC discussions.
- UNESCO: Contributes to cultural preservation tracks.
- OECD: Not a member; opposes Western-dominated guidelines.
- Bilateral agreements: Technology and cyber security agreements with Russia and Iran.
- Regional initiatives: Participates in regional telecom discussions.

12. Human Rights Strengths
- Centralized network controls, enabling rapid detection of cybercrime and security threats.
- High mobile phone penetration, facilitating basic communication channels.
- State-backed efforts to maintain basic telecommunication services during conflict.
- Digital literacy initiatives in state training centers.
- Effective protection of state portals from hostile cyber sabotage.

13. Human Rights Concerns
- Widespread electronic surveillance and interception of private communication data.
- Complete lack of data privacy laws to protect citizens from state monitoring.
- Lack of independent judicial oversight for state surveillance activities.
- Digital censorship and blocking of independent news portals.
- Potential for automated surveillance to target human rights defenders.

14. Likely Questions Against This Country
- How does Syria justify the tracking and targeting of political opposition members through communication intercepts?
- What safeguards exist to protect civilian communication from warrantless state monitoring?
- How do emergency decrees affecting digital privacy align with international standards?
- What checks prevent state-backed cyber groups from launching attacks against civilian networks?
- How is biometric data collected at checkpoints secured from misuse?
- Why is there no independent data protection regulator in Syria?
- How does the state address criticisms of censorship of independent media?
- Does the use of foreign surveillance technologies in policing violate citizen privacy?
- How is digital profiling of citizens used in security files?
- What measures protect children's data in the state's registry systems?

15. Questions Syria Can Ask Others
- How can states defend their digital space when foreign powers use cyber operations to support rebel groups?
- Why do international sanctions continue to block the import of medical and educational technologies, including basic software?
- What measures protect developing states from foreign cyber espionage targeting state ministries?
- How do states with private data markets prevent corporate exploitation of civilian databases?
- Why should global tech companies not be held accountable for harboring channels that promote state instability?
- How do states ensure AI safety standards do not become barriers to developmental technology?
- What steps protect children from digital profiling on global educational networks?
- How do states balance national security with communication privacy?
- What steps support technology transfer to developing countries?
- How can algorithmic bias in global datasets be corrected?

16. Strong Defence Arguments
- The Syrian government operates under a mandate to defend the state against foreign-backed armed groups.
- Any communication monitoring is conducted within the scope of counter-terrorism and national security laws.
- Cyber security strategies protect state infrastructure from external sabotage and cyber warfare.
- Reconstruction of communication networks is focused on restoring basic public utility access for citizens.
- State regulations comply with national emergency decrees designed to restore sovereignty.
- Biometric checks at borders are necessary for national defense and counter-terrorism.

17. Red Lines
- Will not support international regulations that restrict national security operations or internet gateways.
- Rejects any resolution that singles out Syria's security apparatus for criticism.

18. Likely Allies
Russia, Iran, Belarus, China, North Korea, Venezuela.

19. Likely Opponents
United States, United Kingdom, France, Turkey, Saudi Arabia.

20. Suggested Bloc
Sovereign Bloc / Russia-China aligned.

21. Negotiation Strategy
Emphasize national sovereignty, counter-terrorism, and the negative impact of Western unilateral sanctions. Align with Russia and China to resist binding internet regulations.

22. Important Statistics
Over 60% of Syria's telecommunications infrastructure has been damaged or destroyed since 2011, limiting regular digital access.

23. Quick Facts
- Ministry of Communications and Technology coordinates digital reconstruction.
- Syrian Electronic Army coordinates offensive cyber actions.
- Telecom networks are heavily dependent on state-run backbones.
- Lacks independent data privacy watchdogs.
- Facial recognition deployed at government checkpoints.
- Communications are heavily monitored under national security laws.
- Import of high-tech hardware is restricted by sanctions.
- Dependent on Russian and Iranian tech support.
- Lacks a formalized national AI strategy.
- Internet penetration is around 35%.
- Co-sponsored declarations on technology and sovereignty.
- Active in regional telecommunication tracks.

24. Executive Summary
Syria focuses on national security, state survival, and counter-terrorism. Rejects Western criticism of its digital policies as violations of sovereignty, claiming telecommunication control is vital for state defense.
