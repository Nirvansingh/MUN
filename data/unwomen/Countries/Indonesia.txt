AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: GREEN (Core ASEAN Partner / Care Migration Sister Nation)

Why?
• Largest economy in Southeast Asia and fellow major care-exporting nation alongside the Philippines
• Millions of Indonesian female migrant domestic workers (Pekerja Migran Indonesia / PMI) employed across Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and the Gulf States
• Landmark Maternal and Child Welfare Law (UU Kesejahteraan Ibu dan Anak / UU KIA) passed in June 2024: Expanded maternity leave from 3 months up to 6 months under specific health circumstances
• Law No. 18 of 2017 on the Protection of Indonesian Migrant Workers: Banned private recruitment fees and mandated regional government pre-departure oversight
• Major Domestic Battleground: The Domestic Workers Protection Bill (RUU PPRT) has languished in the national parliament (DPR) for nearly two decades without passage, leaving over 4 million domestic workers at home without national labor code protections

Hot Topics
• The newly enacted 2024 Maternal and Child Welfare Law (UU KIA) expanding maternity leave to 6 months
• Decades of civil society advocacy to pass the Domestic Workers Protection Bill (RUU PPRT)
• Transnational protection of Indonesian domestic workers in Malaysia and the Middle East: Bilateral labor disputes and digital monitoring
• Stagnant female labor force participation (~53-54%) and high informality in micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs)
• Community childcare traditions ("Posyandu" community health and nutrition posts)

Expected Debate Frequency: High / Core ASEAN Ally

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Celebrates the historic passage of the Maternal and Child Welfare Law (UU KIA) in June 2024, providing up to 6 months of maternity leave for mothers with health complications and statutory paternity leave.
• Highlights Law No. 18 of 2017: Institutionalizing the "Zero Placement Fee" principle (Perpres No. 90/2020) so migrant care workers do not start their journeys indebted to private brokers.
• Showcases the nationwide network of over 300,000 "Posyandu" (Integrated Health Posts), providing community-based maternal and child healthcare at the village level.
• Emphasizes bilateral diplomacy: Signed the 2022 Memorandum of Understanding with Malaysia establishing the "One Channel System" to protect domestic workers.
• Committed to advancing the 5R Framework and expanding social security (BPJS Ketenagakerjaan) to informal care and domestic workers.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Protect the safety, dignity, and fair wages of millions of Indonesian domestic workers abroad.
• Collaborate closely with the Philippines to form a unified ASEAN negotiating front against exploitative host-country sponsorship systems.
• Maintain macroeconomic growth by supporting women-led micro-enterprises while gradually formalizing domestic work.
• Defend sovereign social policy pacing while expanding national social health coverage (BPJS Kesehatan).

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Maternal and Child Welfare Law (UU KIA, passed June 2024): Guarantees 3 months fully paid maternity leave, expandable to 6 months with medical certification
✓ Law No. 18 of 2017 (PPMI): Overhauled migrant worker protection, requiring village-level documentation, certified skills, and legal assistance
✓ "Posyandu" Community Care Network: Over 300,000 community health posts run by female volunteers monitoring infant stunting and maternal health
✓ Digital Social Security Expansion: Enrolling informal domestic workers in BPJS Ketenagakerjaan (Employment Social Security)
✓ Close bilateral solidarity with the Philippines on diaspora protection diplomacy

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ The RUU PPRT Stagnation: The Domestic Workers Protection Bill has been blocked in parliament for 20 years, leaving over 4 million in-home domestic workers without a statutory minimum wage or labor code protections
✗ Abuse of Domestic Workers in Malaysia & Middle East: Repeated bilateral tensions with Malaysia over rogue recruitment agencies bypassing the "One Channel System"
✗ 53% Female Labor Force Participation: Stagnant for three decades; women bear over 3.5 times more unpaid care work than men
✗ Informality in the Care Sector: Over 60% of female employment is informal, lacking paid maternity benefits or pension security
✗ High Maternal Mortality Ratio: Approximately 173 per 100,000 live births, relatively high compared to regional peers

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 4+ Million: Domestic workers inside Indonesia operating without a dedicated national domestic workers law (awaiting RUU PPRT).
• 6 Months: Maximum potential maternity leave under the newly enacted 2024 UU KIA law.
• 53.4%: Female labor force participation rate in Indonesia.
• 300,000+: "Posyandu" community health centers active across the Indonesian archipelago.
• 20 Years: Time the Domestic Workers Protection Bill (RUU PPRT) has languished in the national legislature.

PHILIPPINES-INDONESIA BLOC ALLIANCE
-----------------------------------
• Unbreakable ASEAN Sisterhood: The Philippines and Indonesia are the two largest care-exporting nations in Asia. When they speak with one voice, destination countries in East Asia and the Middle East are forced to negotiate.
• The Philippines must encourage Indonesia to pass its domestic RUU PPRT bill (modeled on the Philippine Batas Kasambahay), while partnering with Indonesia to lead the committee's working paper on migrant domestic worker standards.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "Indonesia and the Philippines share a sacred bond: our daughters and mothers care for families across the globe. Together, we declare that our care workers are not cheap labor—they are skilled human beings who deserve equal labor rights and complete protection from debt bondage."
• "We urge destination countries: you cannot negotiate with us separately to lower labor standards. ASEAN stands united in demanding the abolition of exploitative sponsorship rules."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Joint ASEAN-led Model Standard for Bilateral Labor Agreements incorporating zero placement fees, electronic wage tracking, and mandatory joint liability for recruiters.
• Expansion of community-based childcare networks (modeled on Posyandu) integrated with national social protection grants.
