COUNTRY-SPECIFIC AI, SURVEILLANCE & DATA PRIVACY POSITIONS
====================================================================

COUNTRY NAME: UAE

1. Country Overview
- Government: Federal constitutional monarchy (federation of seven emirates)
- Political System: Federal presidential system with strong centralized executive under Abu Dhabi leadership
- Global Influence: High (global finance, logistics and energy hub; major sovereign wealth funds)
- Technology Sector: Among the most ambitious globally — G42, TII, MBZUAI, Smart Dubai, AI & Advanced Technology Council (AATC)

2. Official Position on AI
Seeks to become a global leader in AI under the National AI Strategy 2031. Emphasizes innovation, economic diversification and smart-city transformation while maintaining strong state authority over data and security systems. Champions multi-stakeholder, ethics-based AI governance and open-source development.

3. National AI Strategy
- National AI plans: National Strategy for AI 2031, coordinated by the AI & Advanced Technology Council (AATC).
- Government initiatives: Sovereign AI company G42, TII Falcon open-source models, MBZUAI research programs, autonomous transport strategy in Dubai.
- AI priorities: Becoming a top-10 global AI economy, training national talent, building sovereign compute infrastructure.

4. AI Governance
- AI regulation: Sector-based oversight; AATC sets national direction; free zones (DIFC/ADGM) operate complementary regimes.
- AI ethics: The UAE was an early supporter of UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and has actively participated in international discussions on ethical AI governance.
- Government oversight: Highly centralized under Abu Dhabi leadership and AATC.

5. Data Privacy
- Privacy laws: Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (PDPL), in force since 2022; Dubai Data Law 26/2015; GDPR-aligned DIFC Law 5/2020 and ADGM Regulations.
- Data protection authority: Enforcement fragmented across sector regulators; no single fully independent federal authority.
- Citizen protections: PDPL sets consent, purpose-limitation and data-subject rights; free-zone regimes offer GDPR-level rights.
- Cross-border data rules: Federal law requires data localization with limited transfer conditions; free zones allow international flows.

6. Mass Digital Surveillance
- CCTV: Extensive networks in Dubai, Abu Dhabi and transit systems.
- Facial recognition: "Falcon Eye" AI surveillance integrates facial recognition and predictive analytics; smart gates at borders use facial & iris recognition.
- Internet monitoring: State filters content and restricts VoIP and VPN services under cybercrime law.
- Government surveillance: Operated by security agencies with broad executive authority.
- Public justification: Essential for public safety, counter-terrorism and border security.
- International criticism: Allegations involving surveillance of activists, journalists, or dissidents should be discussed separately under Pegasus spyware or targeted digital surveillance, not Falcon Eye.

7. AI in Public Services
- Education: MBZUAI graduate programs, digital learning platforms, AI olympiads.
- Healthcare: AI diagnostics, virtual health services and biobank research in Abu Dhabi.
- Transportation: Autonomous transit strategy, smart traffic management, driverless metro systems.
- Judiciary: Digital court services and automated case processing pilots.
- Public administration: Smart Dubai and UAE PASS digital identity automating government services.

8. AI in Defence
- Military AI: Investment in autonomous systems, border surveillance and drone logistics.
- Autonomous systems: Advanced drone and ISR (intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance) capabilities.
- Cyber defence: Coordinated by national cybersecurity bodies protecting critical infrastructure.
- National security applications: Surveillance, threat prediction and border monitoring.

9. Cybersecurity
- National cyber strategy: National Cybersecurity Strategy focused on protecting critical infrastructure and government systems.
- Cyber agencies: National Electronic Security Authority (NESA) and related bodies; Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 (Cybercrime).
- Cyber threats: State-sponsored espionage, ransomware and attacks on critical infrastructure.

10. Major Technology Companies
G42, Technology Innovation Institute (TII), e& (Etisalat), du, DarkMatter — a company that has faced significant international controversy regarding cyber operations and allegations involving former employees, Core42, Falcon (TII), Cerebras (partnership).

11. International Cooperation
- UN: Active in UNGA and UNHRC discussions; an early supporter of UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence.
- UNESCO: An early supporter of UNESCO's Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence.
- OECD: Non-member; participates in international AI dialogues.
- Bilateral agreements: Major AI and compute partnerships (Microsoft, Cerebras, OpenAI-adjacent deals via G42).
- Regional initiatives: Champions AI and digital economy tracks within the Arab League and GCC.

12. Human Rights Strengths
- First national Ministry of AI and flagship AI university (MBZUAI).
- Open-source Falcon models advancing transparent AI.
- GDPR-aligned data regimes in DIFC and ADGM protecting residents.
- World-class e-government improving service access.
- Major humanitarian donor and technology-transfer partner to developing states.

13. Human Rights Concerns
- "Falcon Eye" AI surveillance with limited independent oversight.
- Pegasus spyware procurement and documented targeting of journalists/activists.
- Broad national security exemptions in the PDPL.
- Criminalization of VPN use and heavy internet filtering.
- Migrant workers (~90% of population) lack robust data protections.
- No single fully independent federal data protection authority.

- Falcon open-source models demonstrate genuine commitment to transparent, auditable AI.
- Smart-city and border technologies improve safety, efficiency and quality of life.
- Free-zone regimes (DIFC/ADGM) provide strong, enforceable data rights for residents.
- National AI investment funds education, talent and ethics — not just surveillance.
- E-government platforms have streamlined services for millions of residents and expatriates.

17. Red Lines

- Rejects proposals mandating independent external audits of state security systems.
- Opposes blanket bans on facial recognition or surveillance technology.

19. Likely Opponents

21. Negotiation Strategy

22. Important Statistics
The UAE was the first country to appoint a Minister of AI (2017) and hosts the world's first graduate AI university (MBZUAI, 2019); ~90% of its population are expatriates whose data rights remain a central concern.

Its federal PDPL and GDPR-aligned free-zone regimes project privacy leadership, yet broad security exemptions, Falcon Eye surveillance and Pegasus procurement leave it vulnerable to human rights scrutiny.
