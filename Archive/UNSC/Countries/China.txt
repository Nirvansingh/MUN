NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Securing maritime trade routes, particularly the Malacca Strait ("Malacca Dilemma").
• Promoting the Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) to build land and sea logistics networks.
• Opposing Western unilateral sanctions that disrupt global trade and economic development.
• Expanding naval influence in the South China Sea to secure regional trade gates.
• Securing long-term supplies of energy, food, and critical minerals.
• Maintaining national sovereignty over Taiwan and surrounding maritime zones.
• Protecting its global dominance in shipping infrastructure and port construction.
• Promoting the use of the Renminbi (RMB) in international trade settlements.

---------------------------------------------------------------

POSITION ON THE AGENDA
----------------------
• Supply Chains: Advocates for open, stable, and secure global supply chains. Opposes Western "de-coupling" and "friend-shoring" as protectionism that violates WTO rules.
• Maritime Security: Emphasizes respect for national sovereignty and maritime boundaries. Promotes cooperative security but opposes unilateral military coalitions (like Operation Prosperity Guardian).
• Free Trade: Strongly supports multilateralism and the WTO. Advocates for lowering trade barriers, except where domestic strategic interests require protection.
• Sanctions: Consistently opposes unilateral sanctions not authorized by the UNSC, arguing they damage global supply chains.
• International Cooperation: Promotes cooperation through the UN, BRI, SCO, and BRICS to build resilient transport corridors.

---------------------------------------------------------------

HOT TOPICS
----------
• Western supply chain de-risking and semiconductor export bans.
• Freedom of navigation in the South China Sea and Taiwan Strait.
• Houthi attacks in the Red Sea and China's diplomatic stance.
• Construction and ownership of strategic ports globally under the BRI.
• US-China tariff disputes and WTO compliance.
• Impact of geopolitical conflicts on energy transit routes to Asia.

---------------------------------------------------------------

STRENGTHS
---------
✓ Largest merchant shipping fleet and world-leading port infrastructure.
✓ World's largest manufacturer and exporter, central to global supply chains.
✓ Controls the processing of the majority of global rare earth elements.
✓ Strategic ownership and investments in ports globally (e.g., Piraeus, Gwadar).
✓ Large, modern navy focused on regional denial and coastal defense.
✓ Deep economic ties with developing countries in the Global South.
✓ Sovereign control over major trade routes in the East and South China Seas.

---------------------------------------------------------------

WEAKNESSES
----------
✗ Vulnerability to maritime choke points controlled by the US and allies (Malacca Strait).
✗ High dependency on imported oil and natural gas (susceptible to shipping disruptions).
✗ Rising labor costs and economic slowdown threatening export dominance.
✗ Geopolitical tensions with regional neighbors (India, Japan, Philippines) over borders.
✗ International concerns over the security and transparency of BRI debt structures.
✗ Dependency on Western markets (US and EU) for high-tech components.

---------------------------------------------------------------

KEY DEFENCE POINTS
------------------
• China's maritime claims in the South China Sea are based on historical rights.
• Unilateral sanctions not approved by the UNSC are unlawful and harm developing nations.
• Decoupling and friend-shoring are politically motivated attempts to hinder global development.
• China protects the safety of commercial shipping lanes in accordance with international law.
• The Belt and Road Initiative promotes global connectivity and trade facility.
• Military presence in the region is defensive and respects national sovereignty.
• China rejects any attempts to internationalize the Taiwan Strait issue.
• Port investments are commercial agreements that benefit host countries.

---------------------------------------------------------------

LIKELY QUESTIONS AGAINST THIS COUNTRY
-------------------------------------
Q1. Why has China not used its influence with Iran to stop Houthi attacks in the Red Sea?
Q2. How do China's actions in the South China Sea align with UNCLOS and freedom of navigation?
Q3. How does China respond to allegations of using BRI port investments as potential military bases?
Q4. How do China's state subsidies for manufacturing align with WTO free trade principles?
Q5. What measures is China taking to ensure that regional conflicts do not halt global rare earth supplies?

QUESTIONS THIS COUNTRY CAN ASK

Ask one strong diplomatic question each to:
