STRATEGIC POI RULES FOR THE PHILIPPINES
==================================================

1. THE THREE-TIER POI STRUCTURE
• The Setup: State a verified fact, international treaty, or official government statistic.
• The Contradiction: Contrast that fact with the target country's lived reality or policy failure.
• The Pin: Force the delegate to answer a closed, binary, or accountability question that exposes their vulnerability.

2. TIMING DISCIPLINE
• Always have the 15-second version ready for standard POI recognition.
• If cut off by the Chair, deliver the 5-second core question immediately.
• If granted a follow-up, execute the prepared trap question that pre-empts their standard scripted defence.

TARGET: UNITED STATES (RED)
---------------------------------------------------------------
• POI 1 (Paid Leave Outlier):
  "Can the honorable delegate of the United States explain how American women can achieve genuine economic mobility when the US remains the only advanced OECD economy with zero statutory federal paid maternity leave, forcing one in four American mothers to return to work within two weeks of giving birth?"
  - Follow-Up Trap: "The delegate cited the FMLA, but does the delegate acknowledge that FMLA leave is completely unpaid and legally excludes 44% of the American workforce?"

• POI 2 (CEDAW & ILO Non-Ratification):
  "Given that the United States champions women's empowerment globally, why has the US Senate spent 45 years refusing to ratify the UN Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), joining only Iran, Somalia, and Sudan in rejecting this foundational treaty?"
  - Follow-Up Trap: "If American domestic protections are superior, why fear international accountability under CEDAW?"

• POI 3 (Childcare Cliff):
  "With childcare costs in the United States exceeding average university tuition in over 30 states and 70,000 childcare centers facing closure due to expired federal funds, what immediate federal funding will the US commit to prevent millions of mothers from exiting the workforce?"

PART 2: PROCEDURAL & POINT OF ORDER PROTOCOLS
==================================================

1. POINT OF ORDER
• Usage: When a delegate or the Executive Board violates the formal Rules of Procedure.
• Phrasing: "Point of Order, Honorable Chair. The delegate has exceeded the established speaking time limit / is speaking on a topic outside the approved moderated caucus motion."

2. POINT OF PARLIAMENTARY INQUIRY
• Usage: To ask the Chair a question regarding the current procedural status or committee timeline.
• Phrasing: "Point of Parliamentary Inquiry, Honorable Chair. Could the Chair clarify the required signatory threshold for introducing draft resolutions under UN Women procedure?"

3. POINT OF PERSONAL PRIVILEGE
• Usage: When physical comfort or audibility is impaired.
• Phrasing: "Point of Personal Privilege, Honorable Chair. Due to room acoustics / microphone static, the delegation cannot hear the speaker."

4. POINT OF INFORMATION TO THE CHAIR
• Usage: To clarify factual or procedural points before moving to a vote.
• Phrasing: "Point of Information to the Chair. Is it within the committee's mandate to establish an independent reporting working group under ECOSOC guidelines?"
