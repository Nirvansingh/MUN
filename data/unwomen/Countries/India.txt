Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: ORANGE (Massive Informal Care & Frontline Worker Disparity)
Delegate: Vaaniya Gupta

Why?
• World's most populous country (1.43 billion) with an enormous, predominantly informal care economy
• Massive Frontline Care Contradiction: Over 2.5 million female community health and nutrition workers (ASHA workers, Anganwadi workers) form the backbone of national healthcare and child nutrition, yet are legally classified as "honorary volunteers" receiving meager stipends rather than statutory minimum wages or pensions
• Over 90% of working women are in the informal economy, entirely excluded from the landmark Maternity Benefit Amendment Act (2017), which provides 26 weeks paid leave only to the tiny formal sector (~8-10%)
• Time Use Survey Data: Indian women perform nearly 6 times more unpaid care and domestic labor than men (spending over 5 hours daily vs 97 minutes for men)
• Over 5 million domestic workers (domestic maids, cooks, nannies) operate without national labor protections, written contracts, or minimum wage floors

Hot Topics
• The legal status of ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activists) and Anganwadi workers: Demands for formal civil service status and minimum wage compliance
• The 90% informality trap: Exclusion of informal care and domestic workers from statutory maternity and social security schemes
• Maternity Benefit Amendment Act (2017): 26 weeks of paid maternity leave with employer-mandated crèche rules versus private-sector hiring discrimination
• The Draft National Policy for Domestic Workers: Years of stalled federal codification
• Rising female labor force participation (from 23% to ~37% on paper) driven primarily by unpaid family farm labor rather than formal, salaried jobs

Expected Debate Frequency: High / Vulnerable on Informal Sector Metrics

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Touts the Maternity Benefit Amendment Act (2017), increasing paid maternity leave from 12 to 26 weeks—the third most generous in the world on paper.
• Highlights the Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY), providing direct maternity cash benefits of ₹5,000 to ₹6,000 for pregnant and lactating mothers.
• Showcases Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0, modernizing over 1.3 million rural child nutrition and daycare centers nationwide.
• Cites the e-Shram portal, which has digitally registered over 290 million unorganized/informal workers (over 53% female) to extend social security portability.
• Celebrates the rising Female Labor Force Participation Rate (FLFPR), which reached 37% in the 2022-2023 Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS).

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Protect national fiscal resources from binding international labor conventions that would require converting millions of community volunteers into full-salaried state civil servants.
• Champion Global South development priorities, demanding international climate and infrastructure funding to alleviate rural female domestic labor (clean cooking fuel, piped water).
• Defend the gradual, phased formalization of its massive informal economy against unrealistic Western regulatory demands.
• Promote the "Digital Public Infrastructure" (DPI) model (Aadhaar, Jan Dhan bank accounts, e-Shram) as the optimal mechanism for delivering care welfare.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Statutory paid maternity leave of 26 weeks for formal-sector employees (one of the longest in the world)
✓ Mandatory workplace crèche (daycare) requirement for all companies with 50+ employees under the 2017 Maternity Act
✓ The e-Shram National Database, digitally registering over 150 million informal female workers for accident insurance and social welfare tracking
✓ Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana: Provided over 100 million clean LPG gas connections to poor households, drastically reducing female time spent gathering firewood
✓ Extensive Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) network operating 1.3+ million Anganwadi centers delivering early nutrition and preschool care

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ The Frontline Worker Injustice: Over 1 million ASHA workers and 1.4 million Anganwadi workers deliver critical grassroots care, yet the state refuses to recognize them as "workers," denying them minimum wages, provident funds, and gratuity
✗ The 90% Exclusion: The progressive 26-week Maternity Benefit Act covers only the tiny formal sector (~8-10% of female workers), leaving over 90% of working mothers in the informal sector completely unprotected
✗ Employer Avoidance: Because the 26 weeks of paid maternity leave is funded 100% by employers rather than a state social security pool, small and medium enterprises systematically avoid hiring female employees
✗ Unregulated Domestic Work: Millions of in-home domestic workers (cleaners, nannies) remain excluded from national minimum wage laws and overtime codes
✗ Staggering Unpaid Care Gap: Indian women spend 299 minutes (5 hours) daily on unpaid domestic care, compared to just 97 minutes for men (over 3 times more)
✗ Distorted Labor Metrics: While the official female labor participation figure rose to 37%, statistical analyses prove this was driven primarily by distress-driven, unpaid family farm assistance in rural areas

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• The 2019 National Time Use Survey revealed that 81% of women engage in unpaid domestic services daily, compared to only 26% of men.
• Women spend an average of 4.5 hours on cooking and cleaning and 2 hours on child/eldercare, leaving virtually zero time for leisure, continuous education, or formal employment.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Anganwadi centers provide essential infant supplementary nutrition, but operate for only 3 to 4 hours per day, functioning as feeding centers rather than full-day care centers that would allow mothers to work full-time.
• Eldercare infrastructure is almost nonexistent outside major metropolitan private nursing homes, leaving the eldercare burden entirely on female family members.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Has not ratified ILO Convention No. 189 (Domestic Workers).
• The Code on Social Security (2020) conceptually mentions gig and unorganized workers, but implementation rules remain stalled, and domestic workers are excluded from occupational safety frameworks.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 2.5+ Million: Total female frontline healthcare (ASHA) and child nutrition (Anganwadi) workers categorized as "honorary volunteers."
• 299 Minutes: Daily time spent by Indian women on unpaid domestic chores and care (vs 97 minutes for men).
• 90%+: Percentage of female workers in India employed in the informal, unorganized sector without statutory maternity pay.
• 26 Weeks: Maternity leave in the formal sector (paid entirely by employers, generating hiring discrimination).

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Boasts of a world-class 26-week maternity leave law while 90% of the nation's women work in fields, construction sites, and homes with zero maternity protection.
• Calls female health workers "angels" and "Corona Warriors" during national emergencies while paying them honorariums as low as ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 per month without pensions.
• Mandates workplace crèches for corporate offices with 50+ staff on paper, but provides zero state enforcement or tax subsidies, leading to widespread corporate evasion.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK INDIA
----------------------------------------------
1. ASHA & Anganwadi Injustice POI: "Can the honorable delegate of India explain why over 2.5 million female community health workers (ASHA and Anganwadi staff), who deliver foundational healthcare and child nutrition across rural India, are officially classified as 'honorary volunteers' receiving honorariums below statutory minimum wage, rather than recognized state workers with full pensions and labor rights?"
   - Expected India Defence: Point to recent increases in incentive allowances, insurance coverage under PMJJBY, and community volunteer frameworks.
   - Philippine Counter: "Calling women delivering professional medical immunization and preschool education 'volunteers' is structural wage exploitation. When will India give its frontline care workers formal civil service status and minimum wage compliance?"

2. The 90% Informality Divide POI: "India takes pride in its 26-week maternity leave law, yet over 90% of working women operate in the informal economy where this law does not apply. How can India claim its care policies empower women when nine out of ten working mothers receive zero paid maternity leave?"

3. Employer-Funded Maternity Backfire POI: "Because the 2017 Maternity Act forces private employers to fund 100% of the 26 weeks of maternity leave, industry surveys show small businesses actively avoid hiring young women. Why has India refused to create a national state-subsidized maternity insurance fund?"

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "You cannot build a modern 21st-century economy on the backs of 2.5 million underpaid women classified as 'volunteers' who keep your healthcare and child nutrition systems running."
• "A maternity benefit law that covers only 10% of corporate women is an island of privilege in an ocean of informal neglect. True care justice requires universal social security for the 90% of women in the informal economy."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International framework requiring the formal labor recognition, minimum wage guarantees, and social security coverage for all community-level healthcare and childcare frontline workers.
• Transition from direct employer-funded maternity mandates to tripartite state social security maternity insurance pools to eliminate female hiring discrimination.
