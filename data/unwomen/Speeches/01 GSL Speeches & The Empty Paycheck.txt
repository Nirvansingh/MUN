==================================================
SPEECH 1: "THE EMPTY PAYCHECK" (FLAGSHIP GSL PERFORMANCE)
==================================================

Total Time Limit: 90 Seconds
Physical Performance & Strategic Silence: 25–30 Seconds
Spoken Body: 50–55 Seconds (~120–130 spoken words delivered at deliberate, powerful cadence)
Target Impact: Absolute room silence, emotional resonance, unassailable moral and macroeconomic authority.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PHYSICAL STAGE DIRECTIONS & TIMED PERFORMANCE SCRIPT
---------------------------------------------------------------

[0:00 – 0:08] | THE OPENING HOOK (Calm, measured, eye contact across the room)
"Honorable Chair, distinguished delegates.
Every single day, across every continent, women and girls perform 16.4 billion hours of care work.
They wake before dawn to haul water. They cook. They clean. They nurse the sick. They care for the elderly. They raise the next generation."

[0:08 – 0:18] | THE PHYSICAL RETRIEVAL (Deliberate, dramatic movement)
[PHYSICAL ACTION: Stop speaking completely. Look down slowly. Reach into your suit jacket pocket or MUN folder. Take out a crisp, folded piece of white paper or an official-looking blank paycheck slip. Hold it with both hands at chest level. Do not speak. Allow the committee to watch your hands unfold the paper.]

[0:18 – 0:28] | THE QUESTION & EXTENDED PAUSE
[PHYSICAL ACTION: Slowly unfold the sheet of paper. Hold it up high in one hand, directly toward the Executive Board and the delegates of the United States, Japan, and Saudi Arabia. Let the arm remain steady. Deliver this single line in a quiet, intense, resonant voice:]

"And after doing all of this... what do they get in return?"

[EXTENDED SILENCE: 6 FULL SECONDS. Absolute silence. Look directly into the eyes of the Chair, then slowly sweep your gaze across the entire committee room. Let the silence feel uncomfortable. Keep holding the completely blank sheet of paper high for everyone to see.]

[0:28 – 0:34] | THE DEVASTATING DELIVERY (Drop voice to an intense whisper)
[PHYSICAL ACTION: Lower the sheet of paper slowly to the podium, keeping your hand resting firmly on top of it. Speak slowly:]

"...Exactly. Nothing.
Not a single dollar. Not a single cent. Not a pension. Not even a written contract."

[0:34 – 1:12] | THE MACROECONOMIC PIVOT (Accelerate cadence, authoritative, commanding)
"This blank sheet of paper represents an eleven-trillion-dollar uncompensated subsidy to the global economy. That is three times the size of the global tech industry, delivered entirely on the backs of women trapped in time poverty.
And when high-income nations face aging societies, what do they do? They import women from the Global South—from the Philippines, from Indonesia, from South Asia—to clean their homes and nurse their parents, while denying them equal minimum wages and separating them from their own children.
The Republic of the Philippines has walked the talk: from 105 days of fully paid maternity leave to our landmark Batas Kasambahay protecting domestic workers, and being the first in Asia to ratify ILO Convention 189."

[1:12 – 1:30] | THE CALL TO ACTION (Passionate, inspiring, closing eye contact)
"The era of invisible, uncompensated care labor must end today.
We call upon this committee to adopt the 5R Framework: Recognize unpaid care in GDP accounts, Reduce time poverty through public infrastructure, and Reward care workers with dignified wages and full labor rights.
Let us ensure that no woman on this earth ever receives a blank paycheck for sustaining human life.
Thank you."

==================================================
SPEECH 2: THE MACROECONOMIC OPENING GSL (POLICY-HEAVY)
==================================================

Speaking Time: 90 Seconds
Tone: Analytical, authoritative, economic leadership.

"Honorable Chair, esteemed delegates of UN Women.
When economists calculate the Gross Domestic Product of nations, they count the steel we forge, the software we code, and the oil we extract. But they assign a value of zero to the labor that makes all of that possible: the care economy.
Globally, women perform over 76% of all unpaid care work—totaling 16.4 billion hours every single day. If monetized, this invisible labor accounts for at least 9% of global GDP—nearly eleven trillion dollars annually.
Yet, what is the reward for this immense contribution? It is time poverty. It is a 25 percentage-point gap in labor force participation. It is the motherhood wage penalty, and a lifetime pension deficit that leaves elderly women disproportionately vulnerable to poverty.
The Philippines refuses to accept that caring for family must mean sacrificing economic mobility. Article II, Section 14 of our Constitution guarantees the fundamental equality of women and men. We enacted the Magna Carta of Women, mandating 5% of all government budgets for gender development. We expanded paid maternity leave to 105 days, and became the first country in Asia to ratify ILO Convention 189, codifying full labor rights for our 1.4 million domestic workers under Batas Kasambahay.
Colleagues, we cannot solve demographic aging by exploiting migrant care chains or offering token cash handouts while demanding 60-hour workweeks. Together with our working bloc partners, the Philippines will introduce a comprehensive framework to Recognize, Reduce, Redistribute, Reward, and Represent care labor globally.
Let us build an economy where those who care for our world are finally cared for by our laws.
The delegation of the Philippines yields its time back to the Chair."

==================================================
SPEECH 3: THE TRANSNATIONAL MIGRANT CARE WORKERS GSL
==================================================

Speaking Time: 90 Seconds
Tone: Protective, assertive, morally uncompromising.

"Honorable Chair, distinguished delegates.
Behind the gleaming skyscrapers and booming corporate offices of the developed world lies an unspoken truth: their economies function only because millions of migrant women leave their own children behind to care for the families of others.
This is the reality of global care chains. Over 2.3 million Overseas Filipino Workers sustain households across North America, Europe, East Asia, and the Middle East. Our nurses staff your hospitals; our caregivers nurse your elderly; our domestic workers ensure your homes run smoothly.
Yet in too many host societies, these women face legal exclusion. They are subjected to kafala sponsorship systems that tie their legal status to a single employer. They face illegal passport confiscation. They are denied statutory minimum wages, excluded from national labor codes, and banned from bringing their own children to live with them.
The Philippines declares unequivocally: care workers are not cheap, disposable commodities. They are skilled human beings with families and human dignity. Under Republic Act 11641, we established a dedicated Department of Migrant Workers to protect our citizens. Through our 'Triple Win' partnership with Germany, we proved that ethical recruitment, wage parity, and educational co-investment can be the global norm.
We call on every receiving state in this room: if you rely on our care workers to sustain your society, you must grant them equal labor rights, universal portability of pensions, and complete protection from debt bondage.
Yield time to the Chair."

---------------------------------------------------------------

==================================================
SPEECH 4: MID-COMMITTEE BLOC MOBILIZATION GSL
==================================================

Speaking Time: 90 Seconds
Tone: Unifying, action-oriented, coalition-building.

"Honorable Chair, fellow delegates.
As debate progresses, the lines in this committee have become remarkably clear.
On one side, there are those who view the care economy as a private inconvenience—something to be solved by deregulated markets, temporary foreign labor visas, or cash handouts that leave patriarchal corporate cultures untouched.
On the other side stands our coalition: Australia, Denmark, Germany, Hungary, India, Russia, the UAE, and the Philippines. We represent every continent, uniting origin and destination nations, emerging and advanced economies.
Our draft working paper provides realistic, actionable solutions:
First, Denmark's 'Pasningsgaranti'—a universal statutory guarantee of public childcare from infancy, with capped household fees.
Second, Australia's historic reform—mandating retirement superannuation on paid parental leave to eradicate the gender pension gap.
Third, Hungary's recognition of multi-generational care—compensating grandparents who provide daily childcare.
Fourth, the UAE's Wages Protection System—tracking electronic salaries for domestic workers to end wage theft.
And fifth, the Philippines' diaspora framework—bilateral labor agreements guaranteeing zero recruitment fees, passport retention, and ethical recruitment.
Colleagues, we invite every delegation that believes care work is public economic infrastructure to join our working paper. Let us adopt a resolution that turns invisible care into recognized rights.
Thank you."

==================================================
SPEECH 5: CLOSING RESOLUTION SPONSORSHIP GSL
==================================================

Speaking Time: 90 Seconds
Tone: Victorious, statesmanlike, persuasive.

"Honorable Chair, delegates of UN Women.
We stand at the culmination of an extraordinary debate. We have heard the statistics: 16.4 billion hours of unpaid labor, eleven trillion dollars in uncounted wealth, and millions of migrant women sustaining global care chains.
Now, the time for speeches is over; the time for action has arrived.
Draft Resolution 1.1, presented by our coalition, is the most balanced, comprehensive, and practical framework ever introduced on the care economy. It does not impose ideological dogmas; it provides concrete institutional mechanisms:
It establishes the UN Global Care Infrastructure Fund to build community childcare in developing nations. It institutes standardized biennial Time-Use Surveys to measure care in satellite national accounts. It mandates non-transferable parental leave quotas for fathers, and codifies the Employer-Pays Principle so no domestic worker begins her job in debt bondage.
To those delegations still hesitating: a vote for this resolution is a vote to liberate working mothers from time poverty, a vote to give dignity to our frontline healthcare heroes, and a vote to guarantee that our elderly receive compassionate, well-funded care.
The Republic of the Philippines proudly urges a unanimous vote in favor of Draft Resolution 1.1.
Thank you, and Mabuhay!"
