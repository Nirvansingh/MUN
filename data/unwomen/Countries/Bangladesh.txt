AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: YELLOW (Global Garment Engine & Migrant Care Exporter)

Why?
• Global powerhouse in the Ready-Made Garment (RMG) sector: Over 4 million workers (over 60% female), making women the primary drivers of national export earnings
• Critical Daycare Deficit for Industrial Workers: Despite Section 94 of the Bangladesh Labour Act mandating childcare facilities in factories with 40+ female workers, real-world compliance is often superficial, forcing garment mothers to leave infants in unsafe informal care
• Major Exporter of Female Domestic Labor: Hundreds of thousands of Bangladeshi women work as domestic maids in the Gulf States (Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Oman), frequently facing severe abuse, wage theft, and returnee trauma
• Pioneer of female microcredit and financial inclusion (Grameen Bank model and BRAC), currently undergoing a historic democratic transition under Chief Adviser Prof. Muhammad Yunus
• Climate Care Burden: Frequent cyclones and riverbank erosion drastically increase female unpaid care burdens (saline water collection, rebuilding destroyed homes)

Hot Topics
• The 2024 Interim Government transition under Nobel laureate Prof. Muhammad Yunus and labor rights overhauls
• Enforcement of workplace childcare (Section 94) in the ready-made garment export sector
• Tragic abuse cases and repatriation of Bangladeshi female domestic workers from the Middle East
• The Domestic Workers Protection and Welfare Policy (2015): Policy guideline versus binding statutory law
• High female unpaid care burden (spending 7 times more time on domestic work than men)

Expected Debate Frequency: Medium-High

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Asserts that women are the engine of Bangladesh's economic transformation, leading the multi-billion-dollar RMG export economy.
• Highlights the historic legacy of Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus and microfinance institutions (Grameen, BRAC) in giving poor rural women financial self-reliance.
• Cites Section 94 of the Bangladesh Labour Act: Mandatory day care centers in all commercial establishments with 40 or more female workers, and the Day Care Act 2021 establishing licensing rules.
• Committed to reforming overseas migration governance under the Overseas Employment and Migrants Act (2013), enforcing welfare tracking for female domestic workers in the Middle East.
• Demands global climate finance, noting that climate change multiplies the unpaid domestic care workload of women in coastal delta regions.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Protect export competitiveness in the global textile trade while improving social compliance and factory childcare standards.
• Safeguard the rights and remittances of female domestic workers in the Middle East without triggering unilateral deployment cancellations.
• Attract international donor funding for climate-resilient rural water supply and community early childhood centers.
• Foster close cooperation with fellow care-exporting nations, specifically the Philippines.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Ready-Made Garment (RMG) Revolution: Empowered millions of working-class women with direct cash wages and financial autonomy
✓ Section 94 of the Labour Act & Daycare Act 2021: Statutory legal mandate for factory crèches and childcare registration
✓ Pioneer in Female Financial Inclusion: Microcredit networks (Grameen Bank, BRAC) have reached over 30 million women with micro-loans
✓ Gender parity in primary and secondary school enrollment achieved through the Female Secondary School Stipend Programme
✓ Significant reduction in maternal mortality over the past two decades through community health clinics

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Superficial Factory Childcare: Thousands of garment factories operate token, unstaffed childcare rooms merely to pass export brand audits, leaving working mothers stranded
✗ Extreme Abuse of Migrant Maids: High rates of severe physical abuse, confinement, and unpaid wages reported by female domestic workers returning from Saudi Arabia and Jordan
✗ Non-Binding Domestic Worker Rules: The Domestic Workers Protection and Welfare Policy (2015) is merely an administrative guideline, not an enforceable statutory act with criminal penalties
✗ 7:1 Unpaid Care Disparity: Bangladeshi women perform an average of 7 times more unpaid domestic care labor than men
✗ Rampant Child Marriage: Despite legal prohibitions, over 50% of girls are married before age 18, cutting short their education and locking them into early domestic unpaid labor

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 4+ Million: Workers in the garment export sector (over 60% women).
• 7x: Ratio of time spent by women on unpaid domestic and care work compared to men.
• 2021: Year the Daycare Act was passed to establish national registration standards for childcare centers.
• 50%+: Proportion of girls married before age 18 in Bangladesh.

PHILIPPINES-BANGLADESH RELATIONS
--------------------------------
• Strong solidarity on migrant worker welfare: Both nations send care and domestic workers to the Gulf and face identical challenges with kafala systems and unscrupulous recruitment brokers.
• Bangladesh can learn from the Philippines' mandatory POEA/DMW standard contracts and Batas Kasambahay statutory model.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "Millions of women in Bangladesh weave the clothes the world wears, yet when they return to their homes, they carry the entire crushing burden of unpaid care labor without affordable nurseries or clean water."
• "We urge destination countries: our women go abroad to earn an honest living caring for your households. They are not disposable property. We demand an end to abusive sponsorship systems across the Middle East."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International certification standards requiring multinational retail brands to co-fund independent, high-quality childcare centers in export garment factories.
• Multilateral adoption of emergency repatriation and legal defense funds for abused transnational domestic workers.
