UNSC RESOURCE GUIDE: KEY MARITIME LAW CONVENTIONS
================================================================

This document outlines key conventions in international maritime law that govern strait transit, shipping safety, and pollution prevention.

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE MONTREUX CONVENTION (1936)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Key Focus: Regulates transit through the Turkish Straits (Bosporus and Dardanelles).
• Key Rules:
  - Guarantees complete freedom of passage for civilian vessels in peace and war.
  - Limits the tonnage and duration of stay for non-Black Sea military warships (maximum 21 days, maximum 15,000 tons per vessel).
  - Grants Turkey the authority to close the straits to all foreign warships during times of war or when threatened.
• Debate Use: Crucial for debates on Black Sea security, grain corridors, and regional naval deployments.

----------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE SUA CONVENTION (1988)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: Convention for the Suppression of Unlawful Acts against the Safety of Maritime Navigation.
• Key Focus: Suppressing maritime terrorism, hijackings, and boarding of civilian ships.
• Key Rules:
  - Defines offenses including seizure of ships by force, acts of violence against persons on board, and placing destructive devices on ships.
  - Requires state parties to prosecute or extradite suspects found in their territory.
• Debate Use: Cite to argue for international legal accountability for Houthi attacks on merchant ships.

----------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE SOLAS CONVENTION (1974)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea.
• Key Focus: Minimum safety standards for merchant ship construction, equipment, and operation.
• Key Rules:
  - Requires commercial ships to carry active Automatic Identification System (AIS) transponders.
  - Turning off AIS to hide shipping movements (common in shadow fleets) violates SOLAS safety rules.

----------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE MARPOL CONVENTION (1973)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Title: International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships.
• Key Focus: Preventing marine pollution from operational or accidental causes.
• Key Rules:
  - Regulates discharges of oil, chemicals, and sewage.
  - Older, poorly maintained shadow fleet tankers operating without oil-spill liability insurance violate MARPOL guidelines.
