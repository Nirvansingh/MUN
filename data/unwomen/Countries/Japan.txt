AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: RED (Prime Agenda-Based Target)

Why?
• World's most aged society (29.1% aged 65+, projected to hit 35% by 2040) causing an acute care crisis
• Ranked #118 out of 146 countries on the World Economic Forum's Global Gender Gap Index (lowest in the G7)
• Gender wage gap of 21.3%—second widest in the OECD (women earn ~78% of men's wages)
• Over 53% of employed women are in precarious, low-paid "non-regular" employment (part-time, contract)
• Extreme domestic division of labor: Japanese men do only 41 minutes of unpaid care/domestic work per day, compared to over 3.5 hours for women (a 5:1 ratio)

Hot Topics
• Severe shortage of long-term eldercare workers (projected deficit of 690,000 caregivers by 2040)
• The "Taiki Jido" (waiting children) nursery crisis and declining fertility rate (1.20 in 2023)
• Reliance on foreign care workers under the "Specified Skilled Worker" (SSW) and Technical Intern Training Program (TITP)
• Paternity leave take-up gap: statutory leave is world-class on paper (up to 12 months), but cultural pressures suppress long-term take-up
• Massive career penalties for mothers returning to the workforce ("M-shaped curve" legacy)

Expected Debate Frequency: Extremely High

BASIC INFO
----------
Official Name: Japan (Nippon)
Capital: Tokyo
Government: Unitary Parliamentary Constitutional Monarchy
Head of Government: Prime Minister Shigeru Ishiba
UN Women Status: Major Financial Contributor / G7 Member
Care Economy Model: Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI) / Traditional Patriarchal Division of Labor

---------------------------------------------------------------

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Promotes "Womenomics" and policies supporting women's active participation in the economy as an economic imperative.
• Highlights the Long-Term Care Insurance (Kaigo Hoken) system introduced in 2000 as a universal model for public eldercare.
• Offers one of the most generous statutory paternity leave frameworks in the world under the Child Care and Family Care Leave Act.
• Expanding public daycare capacity to eliminate "waiting lists" and subsidizing preschool education for children aged 3 to 5.
• Implementing the new "Training and Employment" system to replace the criticized Technical Intern Training Program (TITP).

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Alleviate the existential economic crisis caused by rapid depopulation and an aging society without opening doors to mass permanent immigration.
• Increase female full-time labor participation to offset labor shortages while encouraging higher fertility rates.
• Preserve social cohesion and domestic cultural norms around family care while modernizing corporate work cultures.
• Protect its international prestige against constant criticism regarding its abysmal gender equality rankings.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Statutory paternity leave framework among the most generous in the OECD (up to 12 months with up to 67% wage replacement)
✓ Long-Term Care Insurance (LTCI / Kaigo Hoken) providing universal socialized eldercare services since 2000
✓ Government target to raise male paternity leave take-up to 85% by 2030 (increased from ~17% in 2022 to ~30% in 2023)
✓ Free early childhood education and care for all children aged 3 to 5 since 2019
✓ Universal healthcare system covering maternal and pediatric healthcare

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Ranked #118 on WEF Global Gender Gap Report (lowest in G7 and among the lowest in East Asia)
✗ Extreme Non-Regular Employment Trap: 53.8% of working women are in non-regular jobs lacking job security, bonuses, and pensions, compared to only 22% of men
✗ Deepest Unpaid Care Disparity in OECD: Japanese men contribute only 41 minutes daily to domestic tasks and childcare, leaving women crushed by the double-burden
✗ Caregiver Deficit & Exploitation: Chronic shortage of eldercare workers; relies on Southeast Asian caregivers under rigid visa categories without path to permanent residency
✗ Corporate "Overtime Culture" (Karoshi): Brutal working hours make it impossible for working parents to balance full-time careers without female domestic sacrifice
✗ Widest G7 Gender Wage Gap: 21.3% gap driven by lack of female managerial representation (only ~11% of managers are women)

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• 85% of elderly home care provided by family members is performed by women (daughters, daughters-in-law, wives).
• "Care resignation" (Kaigo Risyoku): Over 100,000 Japanese workers resign from their jobs every year to care for elderly parents, the vast majority being mid-career women whose career progression is permanently terminated.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Long-Term Care Insurance is running unsustainable financial deficits as the baby boomer generation enters advanced age.
• Childcare waiting lists persist in major urban centers like Tokyo and Osaka, forcing mothers to take extended leaves that derail their careers.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Strictly limits foreign domestic workers, permitting them only in designated National Strategic Special Zones (e.g., Tokyo, Kanagawa) through government-certified agencies.
• Relies heavily on Filipino and Indonesian healthcare and care workers under the Japan-Philippines Economic Partnership Agreement (JPEPA) and Specified Skilled Worker (SSW-1) visas, which historically restricted family accompaniment.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• #118: Japan's ranking on the 2024 WEF Global Gender Gap Index.
• 21.3%: Gender wage gap in Japan (G7 highest).
• 53.8%: Proportion of working Japanese women in non-regular, part-time, or dispatched employment.
• 100,000+: Workers who quit their jobs annually due to eldercare demands (Kaigo Risyoku).
• 41 minutes: Time spent daily by Japanese husbands on housework and childcare.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Boasts of generous statutory paternity leave policies on paper while toxic corporate cultures ensure fewer than a third of fathers take meaningful leave.
• Champions "Womenomics" while over half of its female workforce is trapped in dead-end, non-regular employment without career progression.
• Relies on foreign care workers to clean and feed its aging population while refusing to grant them equal settlement rights or family reunification.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK JAPAN
----------------------------------------------
1. Structural Inequality POI: "Can the honorable delegate of Japan explain how Japanese women can achieve genuine economic mobility when over 53% of working women are relegated to non-regular, temporary jobs with zero pension security, while men perform only 41 minutes of domestic care per day?"
   - Expected Japan Defence: Highlight government targets for female managerial positions and expanding childcare subsidies.
   - Philippine Counter: "Setting targets has not solved the reality: Japan ranks #118 on the Gender Gap Index. Without penalizing corporate overtime cultures and enforcing equal pay for non-regular work, Womenomics remains a corporate slogan."

2. Care Resignation POI: "With over 100,000 workers—overwhelmingly women—forced to resign their jobs every year due to eldercare (Kaigo Risyoku), how can Japan claim its Long-Term Care Insurance is preventing women from being pushed out of the economy?"

3. Migrant Care Workers POI: "Japan relies on thousands of Filipino caregivers under JPEPA and the Specified Skilled Worker program to sustain its eldercare homes. When will Japan grant these essential care professionals equal residency rights and allow them to live with their own families?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM JAPAN
-------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has a low female labor force participation rate and relies on foreign remittances."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines ranks #2 in East Asia on gender equality—nearly 100 places ahead of Japan. Philippine women hold equal executive and managerial power, backed by 105 days of paid maternity leave and the Magna Carta of Women. In fact, Japan's own elderly population relies on the expertise of Filipino caregivers to survive."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "No amount of technological innovation can replace human care. Yet, in nations where care remains an unpaid female duty, economic mobility is crushed under the weight of an aging society."
• "We call upon Japan to dismantle the structural divide between regular male careers and non-regular female care labor, and to treat the migrant care workers who sustain its elderly with full dignity."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International mechanisms to eliminate systemic wage and pension penalties for "non-regular" and part-time caregiving workers.
• Binding multilateral standards for ethical care migration ensuring foreign caregivers receive equal wages and family accompaniment rights.
