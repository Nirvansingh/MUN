• Global leader in semiconductor & high-tech supply chains
• CPTPP & RCEP — major free trade agreement leadership
• Energy & food import-dependent — deeply affected by supply chain shocks

Hot Topics
• East China Sea disputes (Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands)

• Semiconductor supply chain security (Chip 4 Alliance)
• Red Sea shipping disruptions & energy import costs
• Quad cooperation & Indo-Pacific maritime security
• North Korean maritime provocations & GPS jamming
• Export controls on advanced technology to China

Expected Debate Frequency: Very High

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Securing global maritime shipping lanes (99% of import/export volume).
• Promoting the Free and Open Indo-Pacific (FOIP) vision.
• Countering China's assertive actions in the East and South China Seas.
• Securing critical semiconductor and advanced technology supply chains.
• Reducing dependency on China for rare earth elements and critical minerals.
• Supporting international law and strict compliance with UNCLOS.
• Protecting critical infrastructure (ports, undersea cables) from cyber threats.
• Enhancing security cooperation with the Quad and G7 partners.

---------------------------------------------------------------

POSITION ON THE AGENDA
----------------------
• Supply Chains: Actively promotes supply chain resilience and de-risking from China. Cooperates on chip supply lines (Chip 4 Alliance).
• Maritime Security: Strong advocate for UNCLOS. Deploys Self-Defense Forces (SDF) in counter-piracy and participates in joint patrol operations.
• Free Trade: Supporter of open, rules-based free trade. A leader in CPTPP and RCEP negotiations.
• Sanctions: Coordinates closely with G7/US on export controls and sanctions against Russia and North Korea.
• International Cooperation: Promotes multilateral, rules-based governance through the WTO, G7, and UN.

---------------------------------------------------------------

HOT TOPICS
----------
• East China Sea disputes (Senkaku Islands/Diaoyu Islands).
• Safety of shipping in the Taiwan Strait and East/South China Seas.
• Supply chain de-risking and advanced technology export controls.
• Impact of Red Sea shipping disruptions on Japan's energy imports.
• Cyber threats to digital port management and shipping databases.
• Implementation of the FOIP strategy across the Indo-Pacific.

---------------------------------------------------------------

STRENGTHS
---------
✓ Advanced technological economy with global leaders in automation and materials.
✓ Highly capable Maritime Self-Defense Force (MSDF).
✓ World-class commercial shipping fleet and port logistics efficiency.
✓ Lead negotiator in major free trade agreements (CPTPP).
✓ High level of cybersecurity resilience and digital infrastructure protection.
✓ Major aid donor, funding maritime capacity-building in Southeast Asia.
✓ Strong strategic alliance with the United States.

---------------------------------------------------------------

WEAKNESSES
----------
✗ Extreme dependency on imported energy (over 90%) and food.
✗ Constitutional restrictions (Article 9) limiting military deployments.
✗ Demographic challenge of an aging population, shrinking the workforce.
✗ Susceptibility of domestic manufacturing to foreign component disruptions.
✗ Territorial disputes with neighbors (China, Russia, South Korea).
✗ High public debt levels, limiting rapid defense spending expansion.

---------------------------------------------------------------

KEY DEFENCE POINTS
------------------
• FOIP is an open, non-exclusionary framework based on the rule of law.
• Japan's maritime operations comply strictly with UNCLOS and international law.
• Export controls on high-tech are targeted for national security, not trade barriers.
• Port and cybersecurity measures protect supply chain integrity for all states.
• Peaceful resolution of maritime disputes is essential for global trade.
• Japan's defense spending increases are transparent and defensive.
• CPTPP represents the highest standard of open, rules-based free trade.
• Discharges of treated water comply with international safety standards (IAEA).

---------------------------------------------------------------

LIKELY QUESTIONS AGAINST THIS COUNTRY
-------------------------------------
Q1. How does Japan reconcile export controls with its advocacy for free trade under WTO?
Q2. How does the discharge of treated water from Fukushima affect maritime environmental trust?
Q3. Why is Japan expanding its military capabilities under FOIP given constitutional limits?
Q4. How does Japan protect its supply chains from dependencies on US tech standards?
Q5. What steps is Japan taking to resolve territorial disputes in the Sea of Japan?

QUESTIONS THIS COUNTRY CAN ASK
------------------------------
Ask one strong diplomatic question each to:

RED LINES
----------
The country is unlikely to support:
• Any challenge to the UNCLOS framework or freedom of navigation.
• Resolutions that permit non-peaceful alterations of maritime boundaries.
• Any framework that weakens intellectual property rights in technology trade.

---------------------------------------------------------------
