Why?

• Passed the "Cheaper Child Care" reforms (July 2023), lifting childcare subsidies up to 90% for lower-income families
• Major recipient of Filipino healthcare and aged care workers, with strong bilateral ties and legal protections

• Gender pay gap transparency: Workplace Gender Equality Agency (WGEA) published individual employer gender pay gaps for the first time in 2024
• Regional Pacific partnerships and care development funding

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Asserts that care infrastructure is nation-building economic reform, driving female labor force participation to a record 63%.
• Showcases the landmark decision to pay 12% Superannuation on Commonwealth Paid Parental Leave from July 2025, ensuring mothers do not sacrifice their retirement security to raise children.
• Highlights the expansion of statutory Paid Parental Leave to 26 weeks (6 months) by 2026, structured with non-transferable parental quotas to encourage paternal care.
• Promotes the 2023 "Cheaper Child Care" reforms, which cut childcare costs for over 1 million families.
• Supported a historic 15% award wage increase for direct-care aged care workers funded directly by the federal budget.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Strengthen economic ties and bilateral migration frameworks with Indo-Pacific partner states, particularly the Philippines.
• Address critical shortages in aged care, disability support (NDIS), and early childhood education without depressing wage standards.
• Champion transparent gender equality metrics (WGEA reporting) on the international stage.
• Maintain fiscal sustainability while managing the rising costs of the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) and aged care.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Superannuation on Paid Parental Leave (effective July 2025): Landmark reform paying 12% pension contributions during parental leave
✓ Expanding Paid Parental Leave to 26 weeks by 2026 with dedicated reserved weeks for each parent
✓ "Cheaper Child Care" Act (2023) raising Child Care Subsidy (CCS) rates up to 90% for families earning under $80,000
✓ Workplace Gender Equality Agency (WGEA): Mandatory public disclosure of gender pay gaps for all companies with 100+ employees
✓ Federal funding of a 15% wage increase for aged care workers, directly valuing the care workforce

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ 25% Gender Superannuation (Pension) Gap: Australian women retire with approximately 25% less superannuation than men, leading to women over 55 being the fastest-growing cohort of homeless Australians
✗ High Out-of-Pocket Childcare Costs: In major metropolitan areas, childcare centers frequently increase fees to absorb government subsidies, eroding family savings
✗ Shortage of Early Childhood Educators: Low wages and burnout cause thousands of educators to leave the sector, leaving centers understaffed
✗ NDIS and Aged Care Compliance Gaps: Ongoing regulatory challenges in preventing commercial provider fraud while ensuring frontline caregivers receive full award wages
✗ Remote and Indigenous Care Deficits: First Nations communities in remote regions face severe shortages of culturally safe maternal and early childhood services

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Australian women perform an average of 4.3 hours of unpaid domestic and care work daily, compared to 2.4 hours for men.
• Over 2.65 million unpaid carers nationwide; female primary carers face significant lifetime earnings penalties.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Childcare is market-delivered but heavily subsidized via the Child Care Subsidy (CCS).
• Aged care is undergoing systemic overhaul following the landmark Royal Commission, transitioning from institutional models to individualized home-care packages ("Support at Home").

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Significant destination for Filipino nurses, aged care specialists, and community disability workers.
• Strong statutory Fair Work Ombudsman protections ensuring migrant care workers are entitled to the exact same award wages, overtime, and superannuation as Australian citizens.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 12%: Mandatory Superannuation rate that will be paid on Paid Parental Leave starting July 2025.
• 26 Weeks: Expanded duration of Paid Parental Leave by 2026.
• 25%: The gender gap in retirement superannuation balances at retirement age.
• 15%: Federally funded wage increase granted to aged care workers in 2023.
• 1 Million+: Families benefiting from the Cheaper Child Care subsidies.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Introduces subsidies while private childcare operators jack up daily fees, pocketing taxpayer subsidies without passing sufficient wage increases to educators.
• Celebrates high female labor participation while women over 55 face the highest risk of retirement poverty due to decades of uncredited unpaid care.

PHILIPPINES-AUSTRALIA BLOC STRATEGY
-----------------------------------
• Australia and the Philippines share deep mutual interests in the working bloc.
• The Philippines should champion Australia's policy of paying superannuation on parental leave as a model clause for the resolution.
• In return, Australia will support Philippine-led clauses on international ethical recruitment and the portability of retirement benefits for transnational care workers.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "Australia has demonstrated the courage to address the most insidious consequence of the care economy: the retirement poverty trap. Paying retirement superannuation on parental leave ensures that caring for a baby today does not mean poverty in old age tomorrow."
• "We urge member states to look at the Australia-Philippines partnership: an alliance founded on recognizing the invaluable contribution of care workers with equal pay, legal protection, and dignity."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Model Resolution Clause mandating state or employer-funded pension contributions on all statutory maternity and parental leave benefits.
• Multilateral adoption of public pay transparency mechanisms (modeled on Australia's WGEA) requiring private corporations to publish gender pay gaps.
