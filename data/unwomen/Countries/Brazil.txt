AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: YELLOW (Latin American Care Leader with Deep Racial Gaps)
Delegate: Jiara Dholakia

Why?
• Largest economy in Latin America with an enormous domestic care workforce (over 6 million domestic workers, 92% female)
• Landmark Constitutional Amendment 72 ("PEC das Domésticas", 2013) & Complementary Law 150 (2015): Extended full constitutional labor rights, 8-hour workdays, overtime pay, and mandatory unemployment severance (FGTS) to domestic workers
• "Bolsa Família": World-renowned conditional cash transfer program reaching over 21 million families, with payments made directly to female heads of household (over 80% female titularity)
• Deep Racial Care Divide: Over 65% of domestic workers are Black or Afro-descendant women, who earn significantly lower wages and face higher rates of informality
• Shift toward informal "Diaristas" (day-cleaners): Employers avoid formal contracts by hiring domestic workers for fewer than 3 days per week to evade FGTS taxes

Hot Topics
• Equal Pay Law (Lei 14.611 of 2023) enacted under President Lula, mandating salary transparency and equal pay for equal work
• The "PEC das Domésticas" legacy: 10 years of domestic worker rights versus the rise of precarious day-labor ("diaristas")
• Chronic shortage of public daycare centers ("creches municipais") leaving millions of low-income mothers without daytime childcare
• National Care Policy (Política Nacional de Cuidados) drafted by the Lula administration in 2023-2024
• Extreme time poverty of Afro-descendant and favela-dwelling mothers

Expected Debate Frequency: High / Regional Champion

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Formulating a historic National Care Policy (Política Nacional de Cuidados) to institutionalize care as a universal human right and public responsibility.
• Highlights the Equal Pay Law (Law No. 14.611 of 2023): Mandating equal salary transparency reports for companies with 100+ employees and ten-fold fines for wage discrimination.
• Promotes "Bolsa Família": World benchmark in social protection, directing cash transfers directly to mothers to ensure children's school attendance and immunization.
• Showcases "PEC das Domésticas" (Constitutional Amendment 72): Eradicating historical labor exclusions for over 6 million domestic workers.
• Statutory paid maternity leave of 120 days (extendable to 180 days under the "Empresa Cidadã" tax-incentive program).

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Advance the National Care Policy as a flagship social transformation program for Latin America.
• Alleviate extreme racial and economic inequality by elevating the wages and social security coverage of Black and Indigenous female caregivers.
• Secure international climate and social funds to expand municipal childcare and urban sanitation in low-income favelas.
• Champion South-South cooperation on domestic worker rights alongside partners like the Philippines and South Africa.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Constitutional Amendment 72 (PEC das Domésticas): Comprehensive constitutional labor protections for domestic workers including mandatory FGTS severance
✓ Law No. 14.611 (Equal Pay Law of 2023): Strict corporate salary transparency and anti-discrimination enforcement
✓ "Bolsa Família": Lifts millions out of poverty, with over 80% of benefits paid directly to female heads of household
✓ 120 to 180 days of paid maternity leave covered by national social security (INSS), not private employers
✓ Formulation of a dedicated National Care Policy inter-ministerial working group (2023-2024)

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ The "Diarista" Evasion Trap: Millions of domestic workers have been pushed into informal day-labor ("diaristas") working 1-2 days per home, which legal loopholes exempt from mandatory contracts and social security
✗ Racialized Care Inequality: Black domestic workers earn on average 15-20% less than white domestic workers and suffer the highest rates of informal labor
✗ Severe Municipal Daycare Deficit: Fewer than 35% of children aged 0 to 3 have access to a public nursery ("creche"), leaving poor mothers stranded
✗ 2:1 Unpaid Care Disparity: Brazilian women spend an average of 21.4 hours weekly on unpaid domestic and care work, compared to 10.5 hours for men
✗ Minimal Paternity Leave: Statutory paid paternity leave is only 5 days (extendable to 20 days only in "Empresa Cidadã" participating firms)

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 6+ Million: Domestic workers in Brazil (largest domestic workforce in Latin America).
• 65%+: Proportion of domestic workers who are Black or Afro-descendant women.
• 21.4 Hours: Weekly time spent by Brazilian women on unpaid domestic care (vs 10.5 hours for men).
• 2023: Year the landmark Equal Pay Law (Lei 14.611) was signed into law.
• 35%: Enrollment rate of children aged 0 to 3 in early childhood education.

PHILIPPINES-BRAZIL RELATIONS
----------------------------
• Outstanding natural alliance: Brazil's "PEC das Domésticas" and the Philippines' "Batas Kasambahay" are the two premier domestic worker rights statutory milestones of the 21st century.
• Both nations should lead the working paper on codifying domestic worker labor inspections and social security tracking.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "Brazil and the Philippines have demonstrated that domestic workers are not second-class servants; they are workers endowed with full constitutional rights, overtime pay, and social security."
• "However, constitutional amendments must be defended against informal evasion. When employers hire day-cleaners to bypass labor laws, the state must close the loopholes and guarantee universal social protection."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Model Clause closing legal loopholes regarding informal day-laborers ("diaristas"), mandating proportional social security contributions for part-time domestic workers.
• Global adoption of direct cash transfer programs prioritizing female titularity to eradicate extreme household poverty.
