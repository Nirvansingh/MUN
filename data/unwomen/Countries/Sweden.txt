AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: GREEN (Premier Global Benchmark / Progressive Ally)
Delegate: Swara Savani

Why?
• World's gold standard in egalitarian care policy: 480 days of paid parental leave per child, with 90 days reserved exclusively for each parent ("daddy months") to enforce equal care sharing
• Universal municipal childcare with a statutory maximum fee ceiling ("Maxtaxa"), capping out-of-pocket costs at approximately 3% of household income
• One of the highest female labor force participation rates in the world (~81.5%), with near-zero drop-off after childbirth
• High public sector employment providing structured, well-regulated care and eldercare infrastructure
• Minor vulnerabilities: The "Nordic Paradox" (high domestic violence reporting rates despite gender equality), strong occupational gender segregation, and privatization pressures in eldercare

Hot Topics
• Equal parental leave sharing: Swedish fathers take approximately 30% of all paid parental leave days (global high, but still short of 50-50 parity)
• The "Maxtaxa" universal childcare price cap as a global policy blueprint
• The "Nordic Paradox": Persistent intimate partner violence and workplace sexual harassment in an otherwise egalitarian society
• Occupational segregation: Women heavily concentrated in public healthcare, teaching, and eldercare, while men dominate high-paying private tech and industry
• Integration of non-European immigrant women into the formal labor market

Expected Debate Frequency: Medium-Low / Model State

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Advocates for the "Dual-Earner, Dual-Carer" model: Care is a shared societal and parental duty, not a private maternal burden.
• Promotes individualized parental leave: 480 days paid at up to 80% of salary, with 90 non-transferable days reserved for each parent to eliminate the motherhood career penalty.
• Highlights "Maxtaxa": Guaranteed municipal daycare for all children from age 1, with fees capped at a tiny fraction of family income.
• Enforces individual taxation (separate taxation for spouses abolished joint-filing penalties in 1971, incentivizing both partners to work).
• Supports universal access to sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR) globally.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Champion the Nordic care welfare model as the premier international standard for gender equality and economic productivity.
• Prevent backsliding on international human rights frameworks, particularly SRHR and LGBTQ+ rights, within UN resolutions.
• Address labor shortages in public municipal eldercare and early childhood education.
• Defend the country's social model while addressing public concern over immigrant integration into the workforce.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ 480 days of statutory paid parental leave (390 days at 80% wage replacement, 90 days at flat rate)
✓ "Daddy Months": 90 non-transferable days earmarked for each parent; fathers take ~30% of total leave (highest in OECD)
✓ "Maxtaxa" Childcare Guarantee: Universal public nursery placement guaranteed within 4 months of application at minimal capped cost
✓ 81.5% Female Labor Force Participation: Consistently ranks top-3 in Europe and the OECD
✓ Individualized income taxation eliminating disincentives for secondary earners (predominantly women) to work full-time

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Occupational Segregation: The labor market remains starkly segregated; women comprise over 80% of public care, nursing, and nursery staff, which pay lower wages than male-dominated private tech sectors
✗ The Immigrant Care Divide: Significant employment gap between native-born Swedish women (over 85%) and non-European immigrant women (~60%), many of whom remain outside the formal workforce
✗ The "Nordic Paradox": Despite world-leading gender equality laws, rates of reported gender-based violence and harassment remain surprisingly high
✗ Privatization in Eldercare: Scandals in for-profit municipal care homes during the pandemic exposed reduced staffing and reliance on hourly, precarious care assistants
✗ Still Not 50/50: Despite 90 reserved days, mothers still take approximately 70% of all parental leave days, contributing to a persistent 10% gender wage gap

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Swedish women spend approximately 3.3 hours daily on unpaid domestic work, compared to 2.5 hours for men—one of the narrowest gender care gaps in the world, though an imbalance remains.
• Equal division of household labor is broadly viewed as a social norm, reinforced by municipal public care services.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Municipalities are legally obligated to provide preschool ("Förskola") within 4 months of a parent's request. Over 85% of children aged 1 to 5 attend public preschool.
• Eldercare is heavily decentralized to municipalities, providing extensive home-help services ("hemtjänst") allowing seniors to age in place.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• "RUT-avdrag" (tax deduction for domestic services): Provides a 50% tax deduction on domestic cleaning, gardening, and childcare labor to discourage informal black-market employment and formalize care work.
• High proportion of foreign-born workers employed in municipal eldercare and hospital assistance.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 480 Days: Paid parental leave per child.
• 90 Days: Mandatory non-transferable parental leave reserved exclusively for each parent.
• 81.5%: Female labor force participation rate.
• 30%: Proportion of parental leave days utilized by Swedish fathers.
• ~3%: Maximum percentage of household income that can be charged for public childcare under Maxtaxa.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Holds itself up as a feminist paradise while public eldercare workers (predominantly immigrant women) struggle with fragmented, hourly contracts.
• Promotes gender parity while persistent occupational segregation ensures women in public care earn significantly less over their lifetimes than men in private corporations.

RESOLUTION IDEAS
