OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Asserts that the UAE is leading the Arab world in gender equality, legal modernization, and domestic labor protection.
• Highlights Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022: Guarantees 12 hours of rest per day (including 8 consecutive hours), mandatory weekly rest day, 30 days annual paid vacation, comprehensive health insurance, and end-of-service gratuity.
• Showcases the Wages Protection System (WPS): Expansion to domestic workers ensures zero wage theft by requiring electronic salary transfers verified by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MoHRE).
• Promotes "Tadbeer" Service Centers: State-supervised licensing centers that eliminate unscrupulous recruiters and provide standard electronic contracts.
• Celebrates political empowerment: 50% female representation in the Federal National Council and women making up over 66% of public sector employees.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Strengthen bilateral and multilateral partnerships with labor-sending nations, particularly the Philippines and India within the working bloc.
• Showcase the UAE as the most progressive, business-friendly, and socially advanced economy in the Middle East.
• Protect sovereign domestic stability while continuously modernizing expatriate labor governance.
• Foster high-skilled female Emirati leadership in technology, diplomacy, and business.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022 on Domestic Workers: Provides binding statutory rights, written contracts, and clear dispute resolution
✓ Wages Protection System (WPS) for Domestic Workers: Electronic bank monitoring of wage payments preventing non-payment or delayed wages
✓ "Tadbeer" Centers: High-standard state-certified recruitment centers providing temporary housing, training, and 24/7 worker helpline services
✓ Mandatory employer-provided medical insurance for all domestic and healthcare workers
✓ 50% constitutional female representation in the Federal National Council (FNC)—among the highest in the world
✓ Strong, close Bilateral Labor Agreement with the Philippines, ensuring shared electronic verification of employment contracts

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Sponsorship (Kafala) Elements Remain: While softened, domestic workers still require official MoHRE transfer approval to change employers, and leaving a home without reporting can trigger "absconding" complaints
✗ Heavy Dependence on Foreign Care: Emirati female economic participation is heavily subsidized by foreign domestic workers, with almost every household employing at least one live-in maid
✗ Private Household Inspection Barriers: Constitutional inviolability of the home limits labor inspectors from conducting random in-person checks on domestic working conditions
✗ Long Maximum Working Hours: Law permits up to 12 working hours per day (though 8 hours consecutive rest is mandatory), which is higher than ILO standard 8-hour workday norms
✗ Heat Stress and Physical Demands: Agricultural and domestic care personnel face harsh climatic conditions requiring constant monitoring

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Emirati women historically shouldered all family domestic care, but modern wealth and foreign labor have outsourced almost all manual housework and childcare to expatriate staff.
• High female educational attainment (over 70% of university graduates are women) enables Emirati women to pursue high-ranking civil service and corporate careers.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• High availability of private international nurseries and state-of-the-art preschools in Abu Dhabi and Dubai.
• Eldercare is anchored in familial respect, supported by universal state mobile nursing units visiting senior citizens at home, combined with live-in care assistants.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Home to hundreds of thousands of Filipino expatriates, with Filipino care workers highly respected and sought-after.
• Excellent working relationship with the Philippine Embassy, Consulates, and Migrant Workers Offices (MWO).

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 50%: Proportion of female members in the Federal National Council (FNC).
• 100%: Domestic workers covered under the mandatory Wages Protection System (WPS).
• 30 Days: Annual paid vacation guaranteed by law for all domestic workers.
• 2022: Year the landmark Federal Decree-Law on Domestic Workers took effect.
• 0: Permissible recruitment fees charged to the worker (employers legally bear 100% of recruitment costs).

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Establishes strong digital contract laws while some private employers still illegally hold domestic workers' passports without state detection.
• The 12-hour permissible workday remains lengthy compared to international conventions.

PHILIPPINES-UAE BLOC STRATEGY
-----------------------------
• The UAE and the Philippines are exemplary partners in the working bloc, representing the ideal synergy between a progressive Gulf host nation and a proactive sending nation.
• The Philippines should commend the UAE's Wages Protection System (WPS) and Tadbeer model as regional best practices for the Middle East.
• In return, the UAE supports Philippine-led resolutions on standard bilateral labor contracts and digital remittance facilitation.

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "The United Arab Emirates has proven that the Middle East can lead on domestic labor reform. Through the Wages Protection System and the 2022 Domestic Workers Law, the UAE has set a high standard for transparency and worker protection."
• "Together with the UAE and our working bloc partners, we call for universal digital wage tracking and the elimination of worker-paid recruitment fees across all destination states."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• International adoption of the Wages Protection System (WPS) model, mandating digital, auditable electronic wage transfers for all domestic workers.
• Prohibition of all recruitment fees and agency charges levied against care and domestic workers (employer-pays principle).
