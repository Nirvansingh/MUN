These four case studies serve as excellent evidence in debate when arguing for or against digital regulations.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 1: THE SNOWDEN LEAKS & PRISM (2013)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Description: Former NSA contractor Edward Snowden leaked classified documents revealing that the US government, in partnership with other P5 intelligence networks, operated mass metadata and communication collection programs (PRISM).
• Impact:
  - Proven collection of telephone metadata and internet communications of hundreds of millions of citizens without individual warrants.
  - Led to global legal challenges and the creation of the UN Special Rapporteur on the right to privacy.
• Debate Use: Cite to argue that Western democracies also engage in mass surveillance, proving the need for domestic and international judicial checks.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 2: CAMBRIDGE ANALYTICA & DATA SCRAPING (2018)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Description: Political consulting firm Cambridge Analytica harvested personal data from over 87 million Facebook profiles without users' consent to create targeted political profiles.
• Impact:
  - Demonstrated how unregulated commercial data collection can be used to manipulate democratic elections.
  - Accelerated the implementation of the EU GDPR and global data protection bills.
• Debate Use: Cite when arguing for strict limits on data brokers, corporate data harvesting, and the need for explicit consumer consent.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 3: PEGASUS SPYWARE DEPLOYMENTS (2021)
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Description: Investigations revealed that the Pegasus military-grade spyware developed by a private Israeli security firm (NSO Group) was sold to governments and used to infect the phones of journalists, human rights defenders, and heads of state.
• Impact:
  - Highlighted the unchecked global trade in dual-use surveillance tools.
  - Pegasus uses "zero-click" exploits to bypass device security and access all communications.
• Debate Use: Cite when calling for international moratoriums or strict licensing of dual-use technology exports.

----------------------------------------------------------------
CASE STUDY 4: SOCIAL CREDIT SYSTEM PILOTS
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Description: Centralized governments have piloted credit systems that score citizens based on digital records, traffic infractions, and online behavior, which can restrict access to high-speed rail, flights, and employment.
• Impact:
  - Demonstrates the potential for predictive profiling and facial recognition to restrict freedom of movement and access to public services.
• Debate Use: Cite when calling for UN-led bans on automated social scoring and real-time biometric profiling.
