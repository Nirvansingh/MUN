AGENDA: Assessing the Impact of the Global Care Economy on Women's Economic and Social Mobility

==================================================
PREAMBLE & MANDATE
==================================================

The United Nations Entity for Gender Equality and the Empowerment of Women,

Reaffirming the purposes and principles enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations, particularly Article 55, which obligates all Member States to promote higher standards of living, full employment, and conditions of economic and social progress without distinction as to sex,

Recalling the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), specifically Article 22 regarding the right to social security, Article 23 regarding just and favorable conditions of work and equal remuneration, and Article 25 guaranteeing special care and assistance for motherhood and childhood,

Reiterating the obligations under the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW), particularly Article 11 and General Recommendations No. 17 on the measurement and valuation of unremunerated domestic activities and No. 26 on women migrant workers,

Reaffirming the Beijing Declaration and Platform for Action (1995), particularly Strategic Objective F on Women and the Economy, and the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, emphasizing Sustainable Development Goal Target 5.4 to recognize, reduce, and redistribute unpaid care and domestic work, as well as Targets 8.5, 8.8, and 10.4,

Acknowledging the foundational labor standards of the International Labour Organization (ILO), including Convention No. 189 (Domestic Workers Convention, 2011), Convention No. 156 (Workers with Family Responsibilities Convention, 1981), and Convention No. 100 (Equal Remuneration Convention, 1951),

Recognizing with profound appreciation that women and girls globally perform over 76% of all unpaid care work, totaling 16.4 billion hours daily—the economic equivalent of 2 billion individuals working full-time without remuneration—representing an uncompensated annual subsidy of at least eleven trillion United States dollars ($11,000,000,000,000) to the global economy,

Alarmed that this disproportionate burden results in acute female time poverty, depresses global female labor force participation to approximately 47%, widens the gender wage gap, generates an alarming lifetime gender pension gap exceeding 30% to 38% in many advanced economies, and accelerates demographic decline across aging societies,

Convinced that the care economy is not a private domestic matter, but an indispensable public good and macroeconomic pillar requiring systemic state infrastructure, multi-stakeholder partnerships, and cross-border labor protections,

Hereby resolves to adopt the following comprehensive 10-Pillar Framework:

==================================================
THE 10-PILLAR CARE ECONOMY FRAMEWORK
==================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 1: MACROECONOMIC VALUATION & TIME-USE SURVEYS
---------------------------------------------------------------
1. Mandates the United Nations Statistics Division (UNSD), in close coordination with UN Women and the ILO, to establish the Standardized Multilateral Time-Use Survey Protocol (SMTUSP);
2. Urges all Member States to conduct institutionalized, publicly accessible National Time-Use Surveys at least once every three years;
3. Recommends that Member States incorporate Satellite Care Accounts into their official National Accounts systems, quantifying the monetary contribution of unremunerated childcare, eldercare, and household maintenance alongside Gross Domestic Product (GDP);
4. Calls for the universal adoption of Gender-Responsive Budgeting (GRB), encouraging Member States to legally benchmark at least 5% of national and municipal agency budgets specifically for Gender and Development (GAD) and care infrastructure programs, modeled after the Philippine Magna Carta of Women (Republic Act No. 9710).

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 2: UNIVERSAL EARLY CHILDHOOD CARE GUARANTEE & FEE CAPS
---------------------------------------------------------------
5. Urges Member States to codify a statutory "Public Childcare Guarantee" (modeled after Denmark's Pasningsgaranti), guaranteeing every child a licensed public or publicly subsidized early childhood education and care slot from the conclusion of statutory parental leave up to primary school entry age;
6. Recommends that national legislation establish statutory fee ceilings ensuring that out-of-pocket childcare expenditures do not exceed 10% of net household earnings, providing completely free early childhood education for low-income families;
7. Mandates that municipal and provincial zoning laws incorporate childcare infrastructure into urban planning, requiring large commercial and industrial employers with 50 or more employees to maintain on-site crèches or provide direct childcare subsidies to employees;
8. Establishes professional qualification benchmarks and statutory wage parity for Early Childhood Educators (ECEs) to resolve chronic staffing shortages and elevate caregiving to a dignified, well-compensated career.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 3: SHARED PARENTAL LEAVE & NON-TRANSFERABLE FATHER'S QUOTAS
---------------------------------------------------------------
9. Calls upon Member States to provide a minimum of 18 to 24 weeks of fully paid maternity leave with guaranteed job security, fully funded through public social insurance pools rather than direct employer liability, eliminating discriminatory hiring disincentives;
10. Strongly encourages the establishment of individualized, non-transferable parental leave quotas reserved exclusively for fathers or second parents on a "use-it-or-lose-it" basis (benchmarked at a minimum of 8 to 12 weeks at high wage-replacement rates), recognizing the proven success of Nordic father's quotas in reducing the motherhood career penalty;
11. Recommends statutory protections ensuring that workers returning from maternity or parental leave are restored to their exact or equivalent occupational positions without loss of seniority, bonuses, or promotional opportunities.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 4: ELIMINATING THE GENDER PENSION GAP (SUPERANNUATION ON LEAVE)
---------------------------------------------------------------
12. Urges all Member States to enact statutory "Superannuation on Parental Leave" frameworks (modeled after Australia's historic 2025 reform), mandating that statutory retirement superannuation and pension contributions (at standard national rates) be paid on all government-funded and employer-provided paid maternity and parental leave;
13. Recommends the institutionalization of statutory "Care Pension Credits" in national public pension systems, ensuring that periods of full-time unpaid caregiving for children under age 6, elderly dependents, or persons with disabilities are credited as full contributory working years toward retirement pension calculations;
14. Calls for the progressive elimination of joint-income tax splitting systems that penalize secondary earners and disincentivize married women from participating in full-time employment.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 5: MULTI-GENERATIONAL FAMILY CARE RECOGNITION
---------------------------------------------------------------
15. Commends the innovative policy models of Central Europe, specifically Hungary's Grandparent Childcare Allowance (Nagyszülői GYED), and encourages Member States to establish "Extended Family Care Allowances";
16. Authorizes Member States to extend state-funded childcare subsidies, tax credits, and pension contributions to grandparents, aunts, and extended family members who provide verifiable full-time care for young children while parents engage in formal employment;
17. Recognizes the profound cultural value of multi-generational family cohesion in sustaining social stability across both developing and developed societies.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 6: DIGITAL WAGE PROTECTION & ZERO RECRUITMENT FEES
---------------------------------------------------------------
18. Demands the immediate, universal enforcement of the "Employer-Pays Principle," strictly prohibiting any recruitment agency, labor broker, or employer from charging recruitment fees, visa processing fees, placement fees, or debt obligations to domestic and care workers;
19. Recommends that destination countries implement auditable electronic Wages Protection Systems (WPS) for domestic staff (modeled after the United Arab Emirates WPS framework), requiring mandatory electronic salary deposits to verified bank or payroll cards to completely eliminate wage theft;
20. Affirm that domestic workers must retain sole, unrestricted physical custody of their passports, travel identity documents, and personal communication devices at all times, establishing strict criminal penalties for employer document confiscation.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 7: TRANSNATIONAL CARE MOBILITY & "TRIPLE WIN" ETHICAL RECRUITMENT
---------------------------------------------------------------
21. Adopts the "Triple Win Framework for Ethical Care Migration" (modeled on the Germany-Philippines Bilateral Agreement), establishing a multilateral standard for the recruitment of nurses and healthcare professionals;
22. Mandates that destination Member States recruiting international health and care personnel provide dedicated financial co-investments, faculty development grants, and training equipment to the medical and care education academies of origin nations, preventing domestic care deficits;
23. Calls for the universal elimination of tied-employer sponsorship regulations and exit-permit requirements ("khuruj") for domestic and care workers, ensuring the universal right to change employers freely and exit the host state without employer authorization;
24. Strongly condemns visa policies that enforce long-term family separation on care workers, urging destination countries to provide legal pathways for accompanied family life and permanent residency for long-term caregivers (modeled on Canada's Caregiver Pilots).

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 8: FORMALIZATION & STATUTORY PROTECTION OF DOMESTIC WORKERS
---------------------------------------------------------------
25. Urges all Member States to accelerate the ratification and statutory domestic codification of ILO Convention No. 189 (Domestic Workers Convention), ensuring that in-home domestic workers enjoy equal rights under general national labor codes;
26. Calls for standard written employment contracts in understandable languages, mandating daily rest periods of at least 8 consecutive hours, weekly rest periods of at least 24 consecutive hours, paid annual vacation, and statutory minimum wage coverage;
27. Recommends the establishment of fast-track administrative labor dispute conciliation mechanisms (modeled on the Philippine Single Entry Approach / SEnA), resolving domestic worker grievances within a mandatory 30-day timeline without legal costs;
28. Mandates the inclusion of domestic workers in national social security schemes covering occupational injury, sickness, and healthcare (modeled on South Africa's COIDA inclusion and Philippine Batas Kasambahay SSS coverage).

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 9: UPGRADING FRONTLINE COMMUNITY HEALTH WORKERS
---------------------------------------------------------------
29. Urges Member States to review and reform the legal status of female community health and nutrition personnel (such as India's ASHA and Anganwadi workers, and Indonesian Posyandu cadres), transitioning frontline care providers from "honorary volunteers" to formal statutory employees;
30. Recommends that community health workers receive guaranteed statutory minimum wage floors, regularized monthly salaries, workplace health insurance, and contributory retirement pensions;
31. Calls for dedicated international public health funding to provide digital diagnostic tools, protective equipment, and ongoing clinical training for community-based female health workers.

---------------------------------------------------------------
PILLAR 10: THE UN GLOBAL CARE INFRASTRUCTURE FUND (UNGCIF)
---------------------------------------------------------------
32. Decides to establish the UN Global Care Infrastructure Fund (UNGCIF) under the joint administrative stewardship of UN Women, the World Bank, and regional development banks;
33. Mandates that the UNGCIF mobilize concessional loans, public-private capital, and multilateral development grants to finance:
    (a) Construction and renovation of community early childhood education centers in low-income and rural municipalities,
    (b) Rural piped water grids, sanitation systems, and clean cooking fuel infrastructure to directly liberate women and girls from manual time poverty,
    (c) Community eldercare day-respite centers and mobile geriatric health units,
    (d) Digital platforms for informal worker social security registration and portable cross-border remittance facilitation;
34. Requests the Executive Director of UN Women to submit an annual progress report to the UN General Assembly and the Commission on the Status of Women on the implementation of this resolution.
