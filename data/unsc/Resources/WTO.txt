This document reviews the primary articles of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT/WTO) relevant to trade blocks, tariffs, and supply chain security.

----------------------------------------------------------------
1. GATT ARTICLE XXI - SECURITY EXCEPTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Summary: Permits member states to take any action they consider necessary for the protection of their essential security interests:
  - Relating to fissionable materials.
  - Relating to traffic in arms, ammunition, and implements of war.
  - Taken in time of war or other emergency in international relations.
• Debate Application:
  - Cited by the US and partners to justify semiconductor export bans and tariffs on strategic materials (EVs, minerals) from China.
  - Opposing states argue that Article XXI is being abused as a protectionist tool to disrupt legitimate commercial supply chains.

----------------------------------------------------------------
2. GATT ARTICLE V - FREEDOM OF TRANSIT
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Summary: Mandates freedom of transit through the territory of each member for traffic in transit to or from the territory of other members.
• Key Constraint:
  - Prohibits discrimination based on flag of vessel, origin, departure, entry, or destination of goods.
• Debate Application:
  - Cited when challenging border blockades, transport routing bans, or pipeline closures enacted by states during conflicts.

----------------------------------------------------------------
3. GATT ARTICLE I - MOST-FAVORED-NATION (MFN) TREATMENT
----------------------------------------------------------------
• Summary: Requires that any advantage, favor, or privilege granted to a product from one member state must be extended immediately and unconditionally to all other WTO members.
• Debate Application:
  - "Friend-shoring" policies that offer lower tariffs or regulatory ease exclusively to allied states represent a direct violation of MFN treatment, unless justified under Article XXI.
