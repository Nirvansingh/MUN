UNHRC MODERATED CAUCUS TALKING POINTS
================================================================

Use these modular bullet points and arguments to quickly prepare speeches for specific moderated caucuses.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TOPIC 1: REGULATING BIOMETRIC SURVEILLANCE & FACIAL RECOGNITION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested Motion: "Motion for a moderated caucus of 10 minutes with a 30-second speaking time on the regulation of biometric tracking in public spaces."

PRIVACY-FIRST BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• Real-time facial recognition technology (FRT) turns public spaces into continuous surveillance zones, destroying the right to peaceful assembly.
• Studies prove FRT has high error rates, particularly for women and minorities, leading to systemic bias and false arrests.
• Deployments must be banned in public spaces, or subject to strict, prior independent judicial warrants.

SECURITY-FIRST BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• Facial recognition is a critical tool for identifying known terrorists and preventing mass casualty events in crowded transit hubs.
• Smart-city camera networks reduce urban crime, locate missing children, and streamline public safety coordination.
• Surveillance is conducted in accordance with national laws and is limited to public spaces where there is no expectation of absolute privacy.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TOPIC 2: ENCRYPTION BACKDOORS AND INDIVIDUAL PRIVACY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested Motion: "Motion for a moderated caucus of 12 minutes with a 45-second speaking time on state access to encrypted communications."

PRIVACY-FIRST BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• End-to-end encryption is a vital tool that protects journalists, political dissidents, and corporate database security.
• Creating encryption "backdoors" for state security services weakens cybersecurity for everyone, creating vulnerabilities that hostile hackers can exploit.
• Warrantless interception of communications violates Article 17 of the ICCPR.

SECURITY-FIRST BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• Absolute encryption shields illegal operations, human trafficking networks, and terrorist planning from lawful investigation.
• State access is only requested under strict national security mandates and emergency decrees.
• Tech companies must cooperate with law enforcement to provide metadata or access under judicial warrants to protect public safety.

----------------------------------------------------------------
TOPIC 3: CORPORATE DATA HARVESTING & DATA LOCALIZATION
----------------------------------------------------------------
Suggested Motion: "Motion for a moderated caucus of 15 minutes with a 45-second speaking time on commercial data mining and data sovereignty."

GLOBAL SOUTH BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• Multi-national tech monopolies harvest citizen data from developing states without providing local data storage or economic benefit.
• We call for data localization laws requiring citizen data to be stored on servers located within national borders.
• Tech transfer is needed to help developing nations build sovereign computing infrastructure.

WESTERN BLOC ARGUMENTS:
• Strict data localization rules fragment the global digital economy and restrict access to advanced cloud services.
• Consumer data is best protected through GDPR-like frameworks that regulate private data processing while permitting cross-border flows.
• Data localization often serves as a tool for state censorship and surveillance under authoritarian regimes.
