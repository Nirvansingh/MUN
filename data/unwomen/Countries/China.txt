AGENDA RELEVANCE SCORE
==================================================

Importance to Committee: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Targetability: YELLOW (Care Policy & Demographic Transition Target)

Why?
• World's largest population undergoing rapid aging: over 297 million people aged 60+ (projected to hit 400 million by 2035)
• Severe demographic decline: total fertility rate crashed to approximately 1.0, prompting the state to shift from the One-Child Policy to the Three-Child Policy
• Falling Female Labor Participation: Once a global leader under socialist mobilization (~73% in 1990), female labor participation has dropped to ~60.5%
• The "Motherhood Penalty" in hiring: Private employers systematically discriminate against young women due to mandatory maternity leave costs
• Massive rural-to-urban domestic care migration: Millions of rural women work as informal domestic nannies and eldercare workers ("Aiyi") in megacities without urban household registration (Hukou) social security

Hot Topics
• Transition to the Three-Child Policy and state pronatalist propaganda versus women's economic autonomy
• Recruitment discrimination: Employers demanding women sign "no pregnancy" agreements
• The Hukou (household registration) divide excluding millions of rural domestic workers from urban social welfare
• Eldercare deficit: The "4-2-1" family structure (one child supporting two parents and four grandparents)
• Revised Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests (2023)

Expected Debate Frequency: High

BASIC INFO
----------
Official Name: People's Republic of China (PRC)
Capital: Beijing
Government: Socialist One-Party Republic
Head of State: President Xi Jinping
UN Women Status: P5 Security Council Member / Major Global Power
Care Economy Model: State-Led Demographic Planning / Familial "4-2-1" Burden

---------------------------------------------------------------

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Highlights constitutional equality under Article 48: "Women in the PRC enjoy equal rights with men in all spheres of life."
• Promotes the revised Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests (2022/2023), prohibiting gender-based hiring discrimination and pregnancy screening.
• Expanding state subsidies for early childhood education and creating municipal eldercare service networks ("90-7-3" model).
• Provides statutory paid maternity leave ranging from 98 days (national standard) up to 158 days under provincial regulations.
• Rejects foreign criticism of its demographic and civil rights policies, emphasizing poverty alleviation achievements lifting hundreds of millions of rural women out of poverty.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Reverse demographic shrinkage and raise birth rates to maintain economic productivity and pension system solvency.
• Avoid excessive employer-side social welfare mandates that could hurt manufacturing competitiveness.
• Maintain social stability by preventing public feminist mobilization seen as destabilizing to state governance.
• Defend China's development model at the UN while forging alliances with developing member states.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ High statutory paid maternity leave: Minimum 98 days nationally, extended to 128-158 days in most provinces
✓ Revised Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests (2023) outlawing invasive pregnancy and marital status inquiries in job interviews
✓ Massive poverty alleviation program lifting over 50 million rural women out of absolute poverty
✓ High female representation in digital commerce and tech entrepreneurship (women found over 50% of new internet startups)
✓ "90-7-3" Eldercare Framework: Policy goal where 90% of elderly live at home with community support, 7% use community centers, and 3% use institutions

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Declining Female Labor Participation: Dropped from 73% in 1990 to ~60.5% today as state-supported workplace childcare collapsed after economic reforms
✗ The "4-2-1" Demographic Burden: Single-child generation women are crushed under the obligation to care for 4 elderly grandparents and 2 parents while raising children
✗ Rampant Hiring Discrimination: Because employers pay maternity leave costs, companies routinely reject female applicants of childbearing age
✗ The Hukou Segregation: Millions of rural migrant domestic care workers ("Aiyi") in Shanghai and Beijing are denied urban pensions, public healthcare, and school enrollment for their children
✗ 2.6:1 Unpaid Care Disparity: Chinese women perform an average of 2.6 times more unpaid domestic and care labor than men
✗ Suppression of Independent Feminism: Independent feminist groups and social media campaigns ("Me Too", #FeministFive) have been censored and shut down

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Chinese women spend an average of 3 hours and 50 minutes daily on unpaid care and domestic labor, compared to 1 hour and 25 minutes for men.
• Eldercare duties fall disproportionately on adult daughters and daughters-in-law, creating immense psychological and economic stress.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Demise of the Danwei (Work Unit) Daycares: Economic privatizations dismantled free workplace daycare centers, leaving infant care (ages 0-3) almost entirely private and expensive.
• Eldercare facilities face severe nursing and bed shortages, especially in rural townships where millions of "left-behind" elderly reside.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Domestic work is dominated by internal rural-to-urban migrant women. They lack formal employment contracts, working through informal agency brokers without injury insurance.
• Has not ratified ILO Convention 189.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 297 Million: Number of citizens aged 60 and older in China (~21% of population).
• ~1.0: Total fertility rate in 2023 (among the lowest in the world).
• 60.5%: Female labor force participation rate (down nearly 13 percentage points since 1990).
• 98 to 158: Days of statutory maternity leave depending on the province.

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Enacts strict laws banning hiring discrimination on paper, while state enterprises and private tech firms continue to demand female applicants disclose pregnancy intentions.
• Demands families have three children while failing to rebuild the affordable public childcare infrastructure dismantled during economic liberalization.
• Relies on rural migrant women to nurse urban families while denying their own children access to urban public schools under the Hukou system.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK CHINA
----------------------------------------------
1. Hiring Discrimination POI: "Can the honorable delegate of China explain how the government plans to prevent hiring discrimination against women when provincial laws expand maternity leave to 158 days, but leave the financial burden on private employers, incentivizing firms to refuse to hire women of childbearing age?"
   - Expected China Defence: Point to the 2023 Women's Protection Law and labor bureau fines for discriminatory job postings.
   - Philippine Counter: "Fining online job postings does not stop silent discrimination during interviews. Without state-funded parental leave insurance, expanding leave without public subsidies simply deepens the hiring penalty for women."

2. Hukou Divide POI: "Millions of rural migrant women provide domestic care and eldercare in China's megacities. Why does the Hukou system continue to deny these essential care workers equal access to urban public healthcare, pensions, and local school enrollment for their own children?"

3. Declining Participation POI: "Given that China's female labor force participation has dropped from 73% to 60% over recent decades, how does the state plan to reverse this trend while simultaneously pushing a Three-Child demographic campaign?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM CHINA
-------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines has a lower female labor force participation rate (~50%) and relies heavily on overseas remittances."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines provides 105 days of fully paid maternity leave without employer discrimination, backed by our Magna Carta of Women and Batas Kasambahay. Philippine women enjoy equal executive management status and complete freedom of civil organization and labor advocacy."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "Pronatalist state slogans cannot replace public care infrastructure. When a society demands that women have more children without providing universal daycare, it inevitably pushes women out of the workforce."
• "We urge China to eliminate the Hukou divide for rural care workers and fund parental leave directly from state social security, so employers no longer view women as a financial liability."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Establishment of state-funded maternity and parental leave insurance pools to replace direct employer-funded mandates that generate female hiring discrimination.
• Multi-stakeholder protections for internal migrant care workers ensuring equal access to urban social security and public education.
