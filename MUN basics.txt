MUN BASICS – QUICK NOTES (BEGINNER FRIENDLY)

1. Mods vs Unmods

Moderated Caucus (Mods)

Formal but flexible discussion.
The person proposing or chair sets:
Topic
Speaking time (e.g., 30–60 seconds)
Delegates speak one-by-one.
Used to:
Debate specific subtopics
Push arguments
Test ideas publicly
Unmoderated Caucus (Unmods)

Informal discussion.
Delegates move around and talk freely.
No speaking order or anything, proper informal chats.
Used to:
Negotiate
Form blocs
Write resolutions/draft clauses
Key distinction:

Mods = public speaking
Unmods = deal-making + writing. More politicking.






2. Types of Points (Know these cold)

Point of Personal Privilege

Example: “Could the delegate please speak louder?”
Or going to the resteroom if you are meant to ask perms.
Point of Order

Use when rules are not being followed.
Example: “Chair, speaking time exceeded.”
Must relate to procedure, not content.
Point of Parliamentary Inquiry

Ask the chair about the procedure.
Example: “How long is the voting procedure?”
Right of Reply

Request if your country is directly insulted.
Granted at the chair’s discretion.

3. Motions (Core ones)

You’ll constantly raise these:

Motion for Moderated Caucus
Motion for Unmoderated Caucus
Motion to Open/Close Debate
Motion to Enter Voting Procedure
Structure of a motion:
“Motion for a moderated caucus of X minutes, speaking time Y seconds, on the topic of Z.”








How to Argue Diplomatically.
These are how you structure content:

Assertion

Claim you are making.
Reasoning

Why is your claim true?
Evidence

Facts, examples, stats.
Impact

Why it matters.
Simple structure:
Claim → Why → Example → So what do we gotta do about that

GSL (General Speakers List) – In-Depth
Main speaking queue.
Set at the start of the committee.
Continues throughout unless paused.
Used for:
Opening positions
Big-picture arguments
Setting narrative
7. Basic Structure for a GSL Speech
Keep it tight and repeatable:

1. Hook (1–2 lines)

Frame the issue.
Example: “The crisis we face is not a lack of resources, but a failure of coordination.”
2. Country Position

What your country believes.
Clear stance.
3. 2–3 Key Points
Each point:

Claim
Reason
Example
Impact
4. Solution Direction

Broad (not detailed clauses yet).
Signals leadership.
5. Closing

Strong, memorable line. It’s usually nice if it links to the claim made in the start clearly.
8. What Actually Wins You Committees
Ignore fluff. Focus on:

Speaking often (mods + GSL)
Controlling narrative early
Leading blocs in unmods
Writing strong, clear clauses
Being logically consistent
9. Minimal Working Strategy
Get on GSL immediately.
Push for mods you control.
Use unmods to build a bloc fast.
Note every influencial/prominent delegates core points.
Speak like you already lead.
