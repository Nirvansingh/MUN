1. CHARTER OF THE UNITED NATIONS (1945)
==================================================

• Article 55:
  - Exact Text: "With a view to the creation of conditions of stability and well-being which are necessary for peaceful and friendly relations among nations... the United Nations shall promote: (a) higher standards of living, full employment, and conditions of economic and social progress and development; (b) solutions of international economic, social, health, and related problems... and (c) universal respect for, and observance of, human rights and fundamental freedoms for all without distinction as to race, sex, language, or religion."
  - Why It Matters: Foundational constitutional authority of the UN system binding all 193 member states. Establishes that economic and social progress without distinction as to sex is an essential prerequisite for international peace.
  - Debate Usage: Quote when delegates claim care work or gender equality is an optional domestic matter: "Article 55 binds every member state in this room: economic stability and women's advancement are not national choices, but Charter obligations."

2. UNIVERSAL DECLARATION OF HUMAN RIGHTS (UDHR, 1948)
==================================================

• Article 22:
  - Core Provision: Everyone, as a member of society, has the right to social security and is entitled to realization of the economic, social, and cultural rights indispensable for dignity and free personal development.
  - Debate Usage: Rebuts arguments that social security pensions should only be granted to formal salaried employees.

• Article 23:
  - Core Provision: (1) Right to work, to free choice of employment, to just and favorable conditions of work; (2) Right to equal pay for equal work without discrimination; (3) Right to just and favorable remuneration ensuring an existence worthy of human dignity; (4) Right to form and join trade unions.
  - Debate Usage: Critical weapon against countries denying domestic workers minimum wage or collective bargaining.

• Article 25:
  - Core Provision: (1) Right to a standard of living adequate for health and well-being, including food, clothing, housing, medical care, and necessary social services; (2) Motherhood and childhood are entitled to special care and assistance.
  - Debate Usage: Confirms that maternal healthcare and childcare assistance are universal human rights.

3. CEDAW (CONVENTION ON THE ELIMINATION OF ALL FORMS OF DISCRIMINATION AGAINST WOMEN, 1979)
==================================================

• Article 11:
  - Key Mandates:
    * Elimination of discrimination against women in employment.
    * Equal right to work, equal employment opportunities, and free choice of profession.
    * Right to equal remuneration and equal treatment in respect of work of equal value.
    * Right to social security, particularly in cases of retirement, unemployment, sickness, invalidity, and old age.
    * Prohibition of dismissal on the grounds of pregnancy or maternity leave.
    * Introduction of paid maternity leave with job guarantees and social allowances.
    * Encouraging necessary supporting social services to enable parents to combine family obligations with work responsibilities (specifically establishment of child-care facilities).
  - Target Countries: USA, Iran (neither has ratified CEDAW!).

• General Recommendation No. 17 (1991) - Measurement and Valuation of Unremunerated Domestic Activities:
  - Mandate: Recommends that States Parties: (a) encourage and support research on the measurement of unremunerated domestic activities of women; (b) collect statistical data on time-use; and (c) quantify the economic value of unpaid domestic work in national accounts.
  - Debate Usage: Foundational UN legal authority for national Time-Use Surveys and satellite GDP accounting.

• General Recommendation No. 26 (2008) - Women Migrant Workers:
  - Mandate: Obligates both countries of origin and countries of destination to respect and protect the human rights of female migrant domestic workers.
  - Key Destination Country Duties: Lifting restrictions on changing employers; repealing exit-visa requirements; regulating recruitment agencies; providing equal labor protections and access to legal remedies.
  - Debate Usage: Powerful tool when cross-examining GCC states (Saudi Arabia, UAE) and destination economies.

4. BEIJING DECLARATION & PLATFORM FOR ACTION (1995)
==================================================

• Strategic Objective F: Women and the Economy:
  - Action Point F.1: Promote women's economic independence, including employment, and eradicate the persistent burden of poverty by addressing structural causes.
  - Action Point F.2: Facilitate women's equal access to resources, employment, markets, and trade.
  - Action Point F.6: Promote harmonization of work and family responsibilities for women and men, through flexible work, equal parental leave, and accessible childcare facilities.
  - Recognition of Unpaid Labor: Paragraph 206 calls on national and international statistical organizations to conduct time-use studies to measure and value unremunerated work, integrating care into economic policy.

5. 2030 AGENDA FOR SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT (SDGs)
==================================================

• SDG Target 5.4:
  - Exact Formulation: "Recognize and value unpaid care and domestic work through the provision of public services, infrastructure and social protection policies and the promotion of shared responsibility within the household and the family as nationally appropriate."
  - Debate Usage: The exact universal mandate for this committee agenda! Cite constantly as the standard metric.

• SDG Target 8.5: By 2030, achieve full and productive employment and decent work for all women and men, including equal pay for work of equal value.
• SDG Target 8.8: Protect labor rights and promote safe and secure working environments for all workers, including migrant workers, in particular women migrants, and those in precarious employment.
• SDG Target 10.4: Adopt policies, especially fiscal, wage, and social protection policies, and progressively achieve greater equality.

==================================================
6. INTERNATIONAL LABOUR ORGANIZATION (ILO) CONVENTIONS
==================================================

• ILO Convention No. 189 (Domestic Workers Convention, 2011):
  - Historic Milestone: The first international labor standard extending full labor rights to domestic workers.
  - Key Articles:
    * Article 3: Freedom of association and collective bargaining for domestic workers.
    * Article 7: Mandatory written contracts specifying employment terms in an understandable language.
    * Article 9: Right of domestic workers to reach agreement with employer on whether to reside in household; freedom of movement; right to retain travel and identity documents (prohibits passport confiscation).
    * Article 10: Equal treatment regarding normal hours of work, overtime, daily and weekly rest (minimum 24 consecutive hours rest per week).
    * Article 11: Minimum wage coverage where minimum wage systems exist.
    * Article 13: Healthy and safe working environments.
    * Article 14: Social security protection, including maternity.
    * Article 15: Regulation of private recruitment agencies and prohibition of charging recruitment fees to workers.
  - Ratification Status:
    * Ratified: Philippines (First in Asia, 2012), Germany, Italy, South Africa, Sweden.
    * Refused/Unratified: USA, UK, China, India, Japan, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Pakistan, Turkey, Indonesia.
  - Debate Usage: The supreme diplomatic weapon against non-ratifying host countries.

• ILO Convention No. 156 (Workers with Family Responsibilities Convention, 1981):
  - Purpose: Create effective equality of opportunity and treatment for men and women workers with family responsibilities.
  - Key Provision: Prohibits family responsibilities from constituting a valid reason for termination of employment; mandates community planning of childcare and family services.
  - Ratified: Norway, Sweden, Finland, Australia, France, Japan.

• ILO Convention No. 100 (Equal Remuneration Convention, 1951):
  - Mandate: Mandates the principle of equal remuneration for men and women workers for work of equal value.
  - Debate Usage: Essential for pay equity claims in caregiving, nursing, and early education.

• ILO Convention No. 111 (Discrimination in Employment and Occupation, 1958):
  - Mandate: Prohibits discrimination in employment based on sex, social origin, or marital status.

• ILO Convention No. 102 (Social Security Minimum Standards Convention, 1952):
  - Mandate: Sets minimum global benchmarks for medical care, sickness benefits, unemployment benefits, old-age pensions, family benefits, and maternity benefits.
