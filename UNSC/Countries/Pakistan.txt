================================================================
PAKISTAN
================================================================

BASIC INFORMATION
-----------------
Official Name: Islamic Republic of Pakistan
Capital: Islamabad
Government: Federal Parliamentary Republic
Current Leader: Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif
Population: ~240 Million
UNSC Status: Non-Permanent Member (Eligible/Frequent Member)
Major International Organizations: UN, OIC, SCO, BRICS (Partner), G77, WTO, IMO

---------------------------------------------------------------

MAJOR ALLIES
------------
• China
• Saudi Arabia
• United Arab Emirates
• Qatar
• Turkey
• OIC Member States

---------------------------------------------------------------

MAJOR RIVALS
------------
• India

---------------------------------------------------------------

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Developing Gwadar Port under the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC).
• Securing maritime shipping lanes in the Northern Arabian Sea.
• Countering Indian naval dominance in the Indian Ocean Region.
• Maintaining critical trade gateways to the Middle East, Gulf, and East Africa.
• Securing overland transit routes to landlocked Central Asian Republics.
• Attracting foreign investment for domestic energy and transport infrastructure.
• Resisting unilateral economic sanctions that disrupt regional supply chains.
• Securing agricultural and fertilizer imports to maintain domestic food security.

---------------------------------------------------------------

POSITION ON THE AGENDA
----------------------
• Supply Chains: Deeply integrated with China's Belt and Road Initiative. Focuses on regional connectivity and building land-sea corridors.
• Maritime Security: Participates in international counter-piracy task forces (CTF-150 and CTF-151) but opposes unilateral naval coalitions in the Arabian Sea.
• Free Trade: Promotes free trade within regional frameworks, specifically with China and OIC states.
• Sanctions: Consistently opposes unilateral sanctions, arguing they disproportionately harm developing economies.
• International Cooperation: Focuses on cooperation within the OIC, SCO, and UN to build secure trade routes.

---------------------------------------------------------------

HOT TOPICS
----------
• Security and expansion of Gwadar Port under CPEC.
• Regional naval balance and maritime security in the Arabian Sea.
• Counter-piracy operations and escorting of merchant shipping in the Gulf of Aden.
• Impact of Red Sea shipping disruptions on national import costs.
• Infrastructure development linking Central Asia to the Arabian Sea.
• Financial debt sustainability linked to CPEC investments.

---------------------------------------------------------------

STRENGTHS
---------
✓ Strategic location commanding access to the Persian Gulf and Arabian Sea.
✓ Gwadar Port offers deep-water access for large commercial container ships.
✓ Strong naval and technology cooperation with China.
✓ Highly experienced military forces active in international counter-piracy.
✓ Strong diplomatic relations with key Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE).
✓ Dominant transit route for landlocked Central Asian trade.
✓ Active participant in OIC-led economic initiatives.

---------------------------------------------------------------

WEAKNESSES
----------
✗ High economic vulnerability, inflation, and dependency on foreign financial aid.
✗ Dependency on imported fuel and energy supplies.
✗ Security challenges near borders and CPEC infrastructure projects.
✗ Limited domestic financial capacity to expand port modernization.
✗ Lower industrial manufacturing export base compared to regional neighbors.
✗ Exposure of domestic markets to global shipping cost increases.

---------------------------------------------------------------

KEY DEFENCE POINTS
------------------
• Gwadar Port is a commercial initiative designed to boost regional development.
• CPEC does not violate international sovereignty or maritime laws.
• Pakistan's naval patrols contribute to global counter-piracy and maritime safety.
• Regional security must respect national sovereignty and non-interference.
• Unilateral sanctions harm developing states and worsen global supply chains.
• Financial agreements with partner states are bilateral commercial contracts.
• Pakistan seeks peace in the IOR, but will defend its territorial waters.
• Food security must be protected through unrestricted access to agricultural imports.

---------------------------------------------------------------

LIKELY QUESTIONS AGAINST THIS COUNTRY
-------------------------------------
Q1. How does Pakistan secure Gwadar Port from regional security threats?
Q2. Why does Pakistan prioritize CPEC over broader multilateral trade frameworks?
Q3. What measures prevent CPEC infrastructure from leading to debt sustainability concerns?
Q4. How does Pakistan respond to concerns regarding Indian Ocean naval militarization?
Q5. What checks prevent security lapses from affecting merchant transit in Karachi ports?

---------------------------------------------------------------

QUESTIONS THIS COUNTRY CAN ASK
------------------------------
Ask one strong diplomatic question each to:
• India: How does India justify expanding its naval deployments in the IOR without consulting regional neighbors?
• USA: How do unilateral trade sanctions affect economic stability in developing nations?
• China: How can we accelerate CPEC maritime connectivity to aid Central Asian transit?
• Saudi Arabia: What measures are being taken to stabilize oil supply lines during regional conflicts?
• Egypt: How can we coordinate to secure Red Sea commercial transit routes?

---------------------------------------------------------------

RED LINES
----------
The country is unlikely to support:
• UN resolutions criticizing the Belt and Road Initiative or CPEC.
• Sanctions that target energy suppliers or regional trade corridors.
• Maritime security mandates that bypass sovereign coastal state approvals.

---------------------------------------------------------------

NEGOTIATION STRATEGY
--------------------
• Countries to ally with: China, Saudi Arabia, UAE, OIC members, G77 states.
• Countries to avoid confronting first: India (avoid escalating bilateral disputes in committee).
• Possible compromises: Support international frameworks for digital port safety training.
• Main objectives: Protect CPEC investments, resist unilateral sanctions, secure transit routes.

---------------------------------------------------------------

MUN TIPS
--------
✓ Emphasize Gwadar's potential as a global logistics hub for Central Asia.
✓ Highlight Pakistan's history of participating in CTF counter-piracy operations.
✓ Frame CPEC as an economic development project, not a military alignment.
✓ Express concern over the economic impact of global trade bottlenecks on the Global South.
✓ Align closely with China and G77 statements on trade fairness.
