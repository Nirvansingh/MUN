This document provides sample preambular and operative clauses for use during the initial drafting phase of working papers in the UNHRC.

----------------------------------------------------------------
SAMPLE PREAMBULAR CLAUSES (Opening hooks and context)
----------------------------------------------------------------

• Emphasizing that the right to privacy, as outlined in Article 12 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) and Article 17 of the ICCPR, is a cornerstone of democratic societies and individual liberty,

• Acknowledging that the rapid deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and automated profiling technologies offers significant opportunities for public service optimization, while presenting severe risks to civil liberties,

• Expressing concern over the increasing frequency of corporate database breaches and the subsequent commercial monetization of sensitive personal user profiles without explicit consent,

• Convinced that state security monitoring and intelligence interception must operate under clear domestic legal frameworks subject to democratic oversight and judicial accountability,

• Welcoming the efforts of regional data protection agreements (such as the EU's General Data Protection Regulation) in establishing consumer privacy standards,

----------------------------------------------------------------
SAMPLE OPERATIVE CLAUSES (Action-oriented mandates)
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. Encourages all member states to establish independent national data protection boards tasked with monitoring the collection, storage, and processing of personal data by both public agencies and private entities;

2. Recommends that states utilize the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, or equivalent regional models, as a voluntary guideline to assess the safety and ethical impact of public sector AI systems before deployment;

3. Calls upon private technology corporations to adopt data minimization principles, ensuring that user data collected is strictly limited to the transaction or service required;

4. Invites member states to share best practices regarding cybersecurity protocols for critical public utility networks and healthcare databases to prevent malicious cyber infiltration;

5. Encourages the creation of regional tech-literacy programs funded through public-private partnerships to educate citizens on digital self-defense, data security, and consent protocols.
