Targetability: RED (Crucial Agenda & Labor Migration Target)

Why?
• Major destination country hosting over 1.5 million foreign domestic and care workers, predominantly women from the Philippines, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Ethiopia, and Kenya
• Vision 2030 has achieved remarkable gains in Saudi female labor force participation (surging from 17% in 2017 to over 35% in 2023), but this surge is directly enabled by outsourcing domestic care labor to foreign women
• The Kafala (Sponsorship) System: While the 2021 "Labor Reform Initiative" eased job mobility and exit visas for private-sector employees, domestic workers were EXPLICITLY EXCLUDED from these reforms
• Private household workers remain legally isolated: domestic workers do not enjoy coverage under the general Saudi Labor Law, remaining governed by a separate, weaker 2013 ministerial decree
• High-profile bilateral labor tensions with sending nations (including Philippine deployment bans) over wage theft, passport confiscation, and physical abuse

Hot Topics
• Exclusion of domestic care workers from the 2021 Kafala reform package
• Passport confiscation and employer exit-visa requirements for foreign domestic staff
• "Musaned" digital domestic worker recruitment platform: improvements versus persistent implementation gaps
• Outsourcing of the domestic care deficit: Saudi women's economic empowerment subsidized by vulnerable Global South labor
• Recent election of Saudi Arabia to chair the UN Commission on the Status of Women (CSW) in 2024, sparking global debate

Expected Debate Frequency: Constant / Direct Target of Sending Countries

OFFICIAL POSITION ON AGENDA
---------------------------
• Touts the extraordinary success of Vision 2030: Saudi female labor force participation exceeded its 2030 target (30%) seven years early, reaching nearly 36%.
• Highlights sweeping statutory reforms: women's right to drive (2018), dismantling of the male guardianship system regarding travel and business, and anti-harassment laws.
• Promotes the "Musaned" electronic portal, which regulates domestic worker contracts, mandates e-payments to bank cards, and provides complaint resolution.
• Asserts that domestic worker regulations provide statutory rest periods, 9 hours of daily rest, paid sick leave, and round-trip repatriation tickets.
• Rejects foreign interference in national domestic labor regulations, framing reforms as sovereign, phased modernizations.

NATIONAL INTERESTS
------------------
• Protect its international standing and showcase Vision 2030 modernization achievements while chairing the UN CSW.
• Maintain a steady, affordable supply of international domestic care labor to support dual-income Saudi households and public sector growth.
• Defend the sovereignty of its domestic legal system against mandatory international labor conventions (e.g., refusing to ratify ILO C189).
• Prevent unilateral deployment bans and wage increase demands by labor-sending nations like the Philippines.

STRENGTHS (PROS)
----------------
✓ Rapid surge in Saudi female labor force participation: from 17% in 2017 to over 35% in 2023
✓ Abolition of male guardianship over passport issuance, international travel, and commercial registrations
✓ The "Musaned" digital portal standardizing electronic domestic worker contracts and tracking electronic wage payments
✓ Generous state-subsidized maternity benefits in the formal sector (10 to 12 weeks fully paid) and childcare subsidies ("Qurrah" program)
✓ Enactment of the Anti-Harassment Law and Equal Pay mandates under Ministry of Human Resources regulations

WEAKNESSES (CONS)
-----------------
✗ Two-Tiered Labor System: The 2021 Labor Reform Initiative, which allowed foreign workers to change jobs and leave without employer consent, explicitly excluded all 1.5+ million domestic workers
✗ De Facto Kafala for Domestic Workers: Foreign maids and in-home caregivers still cannot change employers or obtain exit permits without their sponsor's direct approval, leaving them vulnerable to forced labor
✗ Widespread Passport Retention: Despite statutory prohibitions, employers routinely confiscate domestic workers' passports upon arrival with minimal state enforcement
✗ Excluded from General Labor Code: Domestic workers are excluded from standard overtime, 48-hour weekly work caps, and independent workplace health inspections
✗ Reliance on Human "Care Import": Saudi female economic mobility is achieved not by redistributing care work within the family, but by externalizing the care burden entirely onto underpaid Asian and African women
✗ Overcrowded Deportation Centers: Thousands of runaway domestic workers face administrative detention in deportation shelters ("Sakan") following disputes or abuse

UNPAID CARE WORK SITUATION
--------------------------
• Traditional patriarchal norms historically assigned all domestic duties to women.
• However, oil wealth and subsidized migrant labor have allowed middle- and upper-class families to outsource virtually all physical care and domestic tasks, creating an artificial separation between Saudi women's career growth and domestic care duties.

CHILDCARE & ELDERCARE INFRASTRUCTURE
------------------------------------
• Government operates the "Qurrah" program (subsidizing up to 50% of childcare costs for working Saudi women in the private sector).
• Eldercare in Saudi society remains strictly rooted in home care, almost universally managed by foreign domestic caregivers.

DOMESTIC & MIGRANT WORKER POLICIES
----------------------------------
• Largest employer of Filipino domestic workers in the Middle East.
• Has faced multiple bilateral crises, including the 2021-2022 Philippine temporary deployment ban triggered by wage arrears and abuses, forcing the signing of an upgraded bilateral labor agreement in late 2022.
• Has refused to ratify ILO Convention No. 189.

IMPORTANT STATISTICS
--------------------
• 1.5+ Million: Registered foreign domestic workers inside Saudi Arabia (over 70% female).
• 35.8%: Saudi female labor force participation rate in 2023 (up from 17.4% in 2017).
• 0: Domestic workers included in the 2021 Labor Reform Initiative exit-visa liberalization.
• 2013: Year of the separate Ministerial Decree governing domestic workers (remains far weaker than the national labor code).

IMPLEMENTATION GAPS & HYPOCRISY
-------------------------------
• Celebrates Saudi female economic mobility while keeping the foreign women who clean their homes and care for their children trapped under restrictive sponsorship rules.
• Assumed the 2024-2025 Chair of the UN Commission on the Status of Women while denying 1.5 million female domestic workers the right to leave their place of employment without a sponsor's blessing.
• Laws against passport confiscation exist on paper, but zero major employers face criminal prosecution for withholding travel documents.

BEST POINTS OF INFORMATION (POIs) TO ASK SAUDI ARABIA
-----------------------------------------------------
1. Kafala Exclusion POI: "Can the honorable delegate of Saudi Arabia explain why the landmark 2021 Labor Reforms, which granted foreign workers the right to change jobs and leave the country without sponsor permission, explicitly excluded over 1.5 million domestic care workers? Why are the women who care for your families denied the basic rights granted to corporate employees?"
   - Expected Saudi Defence: Point to the Musaned portal, wage protection, and distinct household privacy regulations.
   - Philippine Counter: "A digital portal is not freedom. If a Filipina or Indonesian domestic worker cannot leave an abusive employer without that very employer's electronic consent, she remains legally trapped. When will Saudi Arabia abolish exit visas for domestic staff?"

2. CSW Leadership POI: "As the designated Chair of the UN Commission on the Status of Women, how does Saudi Arabia reconcile its global gender leadership with its refusal to ratify ILO Convention 189 and its exclusion of household workers from national labor protections?"

3. Passport Confiscation POI: "Article 12 of Saudi domestic worker regulations prohibits withholding travel documents, yet thousands of domestic workers arriving in Riyadh have their passports seized at the airport. How many employers have been criminally prosecuted this year for passport theft?"

LIKELY ATTACKS AGAINST THE PHILIPPINES FROM SAUDI ARABIA
--------------------------------------------------------
• "The Philippines frequently issues unilateral deployment freezes that disrupt bilateral relations, despite Saudi Arabia providing billions of dollars in remittances and millions of livelihoods."
• Philippine Defence: "The Philippines prioritizes human dignity over remittances. When bilateral agreements are violated, or when our workers face unpaid wages and abuse, our government has a constitutional duty under RA 11641 to pause deployment until safety is guaranteed. We welcome cooperation, but economic remittances will never be accepted as payment for the abuse of our women."

GSL TALKING POINTS
------------------
• "True women's empowerment cannot be built on the exploitation of another woman. A society does not solve the care economy by importing millions of vulnerable foreign women and denying them basic labor rights."
• "We call upon the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, as Chair of the CSW, to lead by example: dismantle the remaining domestic kafala system and grant all care workers the unconditional right to control their own passports and employment."

RESOLUTION IDEAS
----------------
• Universal mandate eliminating exit-permit requirements ("khuruj") for all domestic workers globally.
• Inclusion of domestic care personnel in general national labor legislation, with access to independent judicial tribunals.
